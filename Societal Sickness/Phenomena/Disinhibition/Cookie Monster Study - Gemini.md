# Cookie Monster Study: Deep Dive for Beginners

The term **"Cookie Monster Study"** usually refers to one of two famous experiments in social psychology and child development:

1. **The Power & Messy Eating Study** (Ward & Keltner, 1998)
2. **The Sesame Street Self-Regulation Study** (University of Iowa, 2014)

---

## Study 1: The Power Dynamics Study (Ward & Keltner)

### Core Question
Does having power over others make people less mindful of social norms and etiquette?

### How the Experiment Worked
1. **The Setup**: Participants were put into groups of three to evaluate policy recommendations.
2. **The Power Assignee**: One randomly chosen participant was given power—the authority to assign evaluation points to the other two.
3. **The Cookie Plate**: A plate with **four cookies** was brought into the room (meaning everyone could have one, leaving one leftover cookie).

### Results & Observations
* **Taking More**: High-power participants were significantly more likely to claim the leftover cookie.
* **Eating Habits**: Videotaped observations showed high-power participants chewed with their mouths open and dropped more crumbs.

### Key Takeaway
Power can reduce self-inhibition and make individuals care less about social norms, leading to more impulsive behavior.

---

## Study 2: Sesame Street Delay of Gratification Study

### Core Question
Can watching media clips of Cookie Monster practicing self-control help young children delay gratification?

### How the Experiment Worked
1. **The Intervention**: Preschoolers watched clips of Cookie Monster learning strategies (like singing songs or distracting himself) to resist eating cookies.
2. **The Control**: Another group watched non-self-regulation content.
3. **The Marshmallow Test**: All children were tested to see how long they could wait before eating a treat to earn a second one.

### Results & Key Takeaway
* Children who watched Cookie Monster waited **over 4 minutes longer** on average compared to the control group.
* Executive function skills (such as working memory and action inhibition) can be modeled and taught through educational media.

## Comparison Summary

| Metric | Power Study (1998) | Self-Control Study (2014) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Focus** | Social Power & Behavior | Executive Function & Delay of Gratification |
| **Subjects** | Adults / College Students | Preschoolers |
| **Key Mechanism** | Power reduces inhibition | Observational learning boosts self-control |
| **Primary Variable** | Status / Authority | Exposure to educational clips |
