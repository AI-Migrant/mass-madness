# Participatory Persecution: An Integrated Framework, Historical Foundations, Modern Evolutions, and Measurement Methodologies (2024–2026)

*A consolidated analytical report synthesizing theoretical foundations, recent case studies, digital escalation pathways, legal implications, measurement frameworks, and early‑warning indicators.*

## 1. Conceptual Framework: From Theory to Operational Indicators

### 1.1 Core Definition

Participatory persecution is state-enabled or state-tolerated harm carried out by civilians — online or offline — against targeted individuals or groups. It includes:
- mob violence
- denunciation networks
- digital harassment campaigns
- state‑sponsored citizen surveillance
- collective punishment regimes

> “Horizontal violence… between peers, neighbors, and community members rather than solely between state agents.” (Gemini)

### 1.2 Structural Model (Expanded)

```[ Macro-Level State Apparatus ]
(Ideology, Legal Codes, Propaganda, Surveillance Infrastructure)
                │
                ▼
[ Mobilization Interface ]
(Incentives, Denunciation Systems, Algorithmic Amplification,
 Impunity Guarantees, Digital Platforms)
                │
                ▼
[ Micro-Level Social Action ]
(Cyber-mobbing, Doxxing, Swatting, Pogroms, Vigilantism,
 Material Exploitation, Bureaucratic Compliance)
```

### New Operationalization Layer

To make this model measurable:
- Macro-level indicators
  - number of new restrictive laws targeting groups
  - volume of state hate speech in official channels
  - expansion of surveillance infrastructure
- Mobilization interface indicators
  - spikes in coordinated reporting
  - emergence of state-linked online militias
  - incentives (material, social, ideological) offered to participants
- Micro-level indicators
  - mob formation frequency
  - doxxing-to-offline harm conversion rate
  - proportion of civilians involved in denunciation networks

### 4.1 Measurement Methodology for Digital Participation
#### Indicators
- Coordinated Reporting Spike Index (CRSI)
  - sudden 10× increase in reports against a target within 24 hours
- Bot-Human Hybrid Ratio (BHHR)
  - proportion of accounts with bot-like cadence
- Cross-Platform Convergence Score (CPCS)
  - same target attacked across ≥3 platforms
- Doxxing-to-Offline Harm Conversion Rate (DOHCR)
  - % of doxxing cases leading to physical harassment

#### Data Sources
- platform transparency APIs
- OSINT scraping
- metadata from takedown requests
- NGO incident logs

### 4.2 Automated Actors & Hybrid Bot-Human Campaigns
#### Detection Heuristics
- account age < 30 days
- posting cadence > 50 posts/day
- network centrality anomalies
- linguistic repetition patterns

#### Policy Responses
- mandatory platform transparency reports
- researcher API access
- bot labeling requirements

### 4.3 Legal Thresholds & International Law Mapping

#### State Responsibility
Participatory persecution triggers state responsibility when:
- state encourages or tolerates civilian harm
- state fails to prevent foreseeable mob violence
- state actors coordinate digital harassment (e.g., Burkina Faso BIR‑C)

#### Asylum Law
- systematic blacklisting (China) = persecution
- surveillance + denunciation networks = well-founded fear
- digital persecution → valid asylum claim if offline harm risk exists

#### Criminal Liability
- coordinated online harassment → conspiracy
- swatting → terrorism in some jurisdictions
- deepfake sexual violence → sexual assault analogue

### 4.4 Victim Support & Remediation Pathways
#### Rapid Response Checklist
1. Digital containment
   - emergency takedown requests
   - account lockdown
2. Evidence preservation
   - screenshots, metadata, URLs
3. Legal reporting
   - cybercrime units
   - NGO hotlines
4. Psychosocial support
   - trauma counseling
   - safe housing
5. Emergency relocation
   - for high-risk cases (journalists, activists)

### 4.5 Cross-Border Jurisdiction & Enforcement
#### Mechanisms
- Mutual Legal Assistance Treaties (MLATs)
- standardized cross-border takedown clauses
- platform-level global enforcement protocols

### 4.6 Quantitative Early-Warning Indicators
#### Thresholds
- 10× spike in hate speech in a locality
- ≥100 new accounts targeting one individual within 48 hours
- geotagged threats within 5 km radius
- viral rumor reaching >50,000 shares in 24 hours
- offline mobilization signals (e.g., WhatsApp group formation)
