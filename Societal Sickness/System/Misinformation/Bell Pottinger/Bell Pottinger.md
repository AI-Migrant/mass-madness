### Summary
The investigation reveals how the prestigious UK PR firm was hired by South Africa's Gupta family to run a racially divisive campaign promoting "white monopoly capital" to deflect from corruption allegations, leading to its expulsion from the PR industry body and eventual collapse in 2017.
Top finding: Bell Pottinger's campaign for Oakbay Investments (Gupta family) used fake social media accounts, Wikipedia manipulation, and divisive messaging to stoke racial tensions in South Africa, resulting in the firm's PRCA expulsion — the harshest sanction in the body's history — and its bankruptcy within weeks.

# Bell Pottinger Scandal: The South Africa Racial Tension Campaign and Its Global Fallout

## Question

What was the Bell Pottinger scandal, how did it unfold, who were the key actors, what tactics were employed, what were the investigation findings, and what were the consequences for the firm and the PR industry?

## Executive Summary

1. **Core Allegation**: Bell Pottinger, a prestigious UK PR firm, was hired by the Gupta family's Oakbay Investments (January 2016) to run a campaign that deliberately stoked racial tensions in South Africa by promoting the "white monopoly capital" narrative, deflecting attention from corruption allegations against the Guptas and President Jacob Zuma.

2. **Campaign Tactics**: The firm created fake Twitter accounts, manipulated Wikipedia pages, and amplified the #WhiteMonopolyCapital hashtag to portray Gupta opponents as agents of white-owned businesses. Internal emails (Guptaleaks) revealed plans for "dirty" PR tactics, including smearing political opponents.

3. **Key Findings**: The Herbert Smith Freehills investigation (July 2017) found Bell Pottinger's campaign was "potentially racially divisive and/or potentially offensive" and breached ethical principles. The PRCA expelled the firm in September 2017 for inciting racial hatred, the harshest sanction in its history.

4. **Collapse**: Following the PRCA expulsion, client exodus, and reputational damage, Bell Pottinger entered administration on 12 September 2017, owing £14 million to creditors. Around 250 staff lost their jobs.

5. **Legal Aftermath**: Former partners faced potential director disqualification and £4 million in repayment demands from liquidators. The Insolvency Service ultimately dropped its case in March 2023.

6. **Industry Impact**: The scandal exposed systemic failures in PR ethics, leading to calls for stricter industry regulation and highlighting the risks of reputation laundering for controversial clients.

7. **Legacy**: The case became a global example of PR malpractice, cited in discussions about disinformation, corporate ethics, and the weaponization of social media for political purposes.

## Findings

### Background: The Context

**South African Political Landscape (2016-2017)**

South Africa was grappling with allegations of "state capture" — systematic corruption in which private interests, particularly the Gupta family, were alleged to have influenced government decisions and appointments for personal gain. President Jacob Zuma faced mounting corruption allegations and calls for his resignation. The Gupta family (Ajay, Atul, and Rajesh "Tony" Gupta) — Indian-born businessmen who moved to South Africa in 1993 — had built a vast conglomerate with interests in mining, media, and other sectors, and were accused of using their close relationship with Zuma to secure favorable deals and political influence.

**Oakbay Investments**

Oakbay Investments was the Gupta family's South African holding company. As allegations of corruption and state capture mounted against the Guptas, Oakbay sought to manage its reputation crisis. In January 2016, Oakbay engaged Bell Pottinger, reportedly paying approximately £100,000 per month for PR services.

**Bell Pottinger's Reputation**

Founded by Timothy Bell (Lord Bell), a former PR adviser to Margaret Thatcher, Bell Pottinger was one of the UK's most prestigious PR firms. It had a history of taking on controversial clients, including former Chilean dictator Augusto Pinochet, Syrian first lady Asma al-Assad, and arms manufacturer BAE Systems. The firm was already under scrutiny for previous ethical violations, including Wikipedia editing for clients and a $540 million Pentagon contract to create fake terror videos in Iraq.

### The Campaign: Strategy and Tactics

**Objectives**

The explicit goal, as revealed in leaked emails and investigations, was to deflect attention from corruption allegations against the Gupta family and President Zuma by:

1. Portraying the Guptas as victims of a conspiracy
2. Shifting blame to "white monopoly capital" — a term suggesting white-owned businesses were conspiring against economic transformation
3. Suggesting that white business interests were actively blocking economic empowerment of black South Africans
4. Dividing public opinion along racial lines to weaken opposition

**Key Messaging**

- **"White Monopoly Capital"**: The central narrative, promoted through social media and Gupta-owned media outlets (ANN7 news channel, The New Age newspaper)
- **#WhiteMonopolyCapital**: Hashtag amplified across Twitter
- **Economic Emancipation**: Frame suggesting the Guptas were champions of black economic empowerment

**Tactics Employed**

1. **Social Media Manipulation**
   - Creation of **fake Twitter accounts** to post racial messages targeting opponents of the Guptas
   - Amplification of the #WhiteMonopolyCapital hashtag
   - Coordination with Gupta-associated accounts to create appearance of grassroots support
   - Note: Herbert Smith Freehills investigation found **no direct evidence** that Bell Pottinger itself created or instructed the use of Twitter bots, though the campaign's effect was to amplify divisive content

2. **Wikipedia Editing**
   - Substantial editing of Wikipedia pages about the Gupta family to remove negative information
   - Bell Pottinger employee allegedly emailed content to Gupta accounts for upload
   - Similar manipulation of pages for Oakbay Investments and associated entities
   - Editing of Johann Rupert's Wikipedia page (alleged by Rupert in December 2016)

3. **Content Creation**
   - Drafting talking points on "economic emancipation" for Gupta allies
   - Commissioning advertisements claiming South African banks had threatened Oakbay employees' livelihoods
   - Preparing speeches for political figures, including ANC Youth League leader Collen Maine
   - Nick Lambert (Bell Pottinger senior advisor) prepared "key moments" for a speech including the line: "Those who want to disrupt the State of the Nation speech must prepare themselves for a civil war"

4. **Media Strategy**
   - Attempted suppression of negative Google results about the Guptas through Israeli digital-reputation service Veribo (later renamed Percepto)
   - Coordination with Gupta-owned media outlets to promote the narrative

**Guptaleaks Revelations**

In June 2017, South African newspapers *The Citizen* and *Sunday Times* published leaked emails between Duduzane Zuma (President Zuma's son and Gupta associate) and Bell Pottinger employees. These revealed:

- A proposed "dirty" PR statement by Bell Pottinger employee Nick Lambert, accusing Deputy Finance Minister Mcebisi Jonas of dishonesty and corruption
- The statement was intended for release after Jonas publicly revealed he had been offered a bribe by a Gupta family member
- Legal concerns raised by Victoria Geoghegan prevented the statement's release
- Emails showing Bell Pottinger's direct involvement in crafting divisive messaging

### Key Personnel Involved

**Bell Pottinger Side:**

- **Victoria Geoghegan**: Lead partner on the Oakbay account, dismissed in July 2017. Initially denied allegations but was identified as central to the campaign. Later joined risk management firm Thoburns (2019).
- **Nick Lambert**: Senior advisor, formed the "dirty" statement targeting Mcebisi Jonas. Prepared divisive speech content for ANC Youth League.
- **James Henderson**: CEO and largest shareholder. Issued public apology in July 2017, resigned in early September 2017. Faced potential £400,000 repayment demand from liquidators.
- **Lord Timothy Bell (Lord Bell)**: Co-founder, left the firm in August 2016. Later confirmed Oakbay actions were a factor in his departure. Denied involvement in securing the Gupta business.
- **Chris Geoghegan**: Victoria Geoghegan's father, introduced Bell Pottinger to the Guptas in January 2016. Received £120,000 in fees. Resigned from Rentokil Initial in September 2017.

**Client Side:**

- **Gupta Family**: Ajay, Atul, and Rajesh (Tony) Gupta — owners of Oakbay Investments. Consistently denied corruption allegations.
- **Duduzane Zuma**: Son of President Jacob Zuma, close Gupta associate, involved in email communications with Bell Pottinger.
- **Salim Essa**: Gupta associate, connected to Hamza Farooqui (MD of WorldSpace in South Africa)
- **Jacob Zuma**: President of South Africa, faced corruption allegations and ties to the Guptas. Used the "white monopoly capital" narrative in public statements.

**Complainants and Investigators:**

- **Democratic Alliance (DA)**: South African opposition party that filed complaints with PRCA and CIPR. Spokesperson Phumzile van Damme was prominent in criticizing Bell Pottinger.
- **Mmusi Maimane**: DA leader who called for PRCA to strip Bell Pottinger of membership.
- **Mcebisi Jonas**: Former Deputy Finance Minister who alleged bribe offer from Guptas, target of proposed smear campaign.
- **Herbert Smith Freehills**: Law firm commissioned by Bell Pottinger to conduct internal review (reportedly cost £1 million).
- **Francis Ingham**: PRCA Director-General who described Bell Pottinger's actions as "absolutely unthinkable."

### Investigations and Official Findings

**Herbert Smith Freehills Investigation (July-September 2017)**

Commissioned by Bell Pottinger to review the Oakbay account. Key findings:

- Bell Pottinger senior management "should have known that the campaign was at risk of causing offence, including on grounds of race"
- The firm "ought to have exercised extreme care and should have closely scrutinised the creation of content for the campaign. This does not appear to have happened."
- Certain material "was negative or targeted towards wealthy white South African individuals or corporates and/or was potentially racially divisive and/or potentially offensive and was created in breach of relevant ethical principles"
- **No direct evidence** found that Bell Pottinger "used, or instructed others to use so-called Twitterbots"

**PRCA Investigation and Ruling (September 2017)**

The Public Relations and Communications Association, the UK's PR industry body, conducted its own investigation following the Democratic Alliance complaint. Findings:

- Bell Pottinger breached **Clause 1.1** of the PRCA Professional Charter (positive duty to observe highest standards)
- Bell Pottinger breached **Clause 4** of the PRCA Professional Charter
- Bell Pottinger breached **Clause 12** of the PRCA Public Affairs and Lobbying Code of Conduct
- Bell Pottinger breached **Clause 13** of the PRCA Public Affairs and Lobbying Code of Conduct
- Campaign "incited racial hatred" and was "absolutely unthinkable"
- **Sanction**: Immediate expulsion from PRCA; ineligible to reapply for corporate membership for minimum 5 years
- Francis Ingham: "Bell Pottinger has brought the PR and communications industry into disrepute with its actions, and it has received the harshest possible sanctions"

**Democratic Alliance Complaint**

The DA, South Africa's main opposition party, filed complaints with both the PRCA and the Chartered Institute of Public Relations (CIPR). Key allegations:

- Bell Pottinger carried out a "hateful and divisive campaign to divide South Africa along the lines of race"
- The campaign portrayed opponents of the Guptas and Zuma as agents of "white monopoly capital"
- Bell Pottinger "tried to divide and conquer South Africans by abusing racial tensions in a bid to keep Jacob Zuma and his party, the ANC, in power"

### Consequences

**For Bell Pottinger:**

1. **Reputational Collapse**: Once a prestigious firm with "the most controversial client list" in PR, became globally synonymous with unethical practice
2. **Client Exodus**: Major clients including Richemont (Johann Rupert), Investec, South African Tourism board, and others terminated contracts
3. **Financial Collapse**: Entered administration on 12 September 2017, owing £14 million to creditors including £5.1m to Lloyds Bank
4. **Staff Impact**: Approximately 250 employees and partners lost their jobs; regional operations in Middle East and Asia split away and rebranded
5. **Leadership Departures**: James Henderson (CEO) resigned, Lord Bell had already left in August 2016, Victoria Geoghegan dismissed
6. **Legal Fallout**: Liquidators (BDO) pursued former partners for £4 million in repayments; potential director disqualification proceedings initiated

**For the PR Industry:**

1. **Regulatory Impact**: PRCA demonstrated willingness to impose harshest sanctions on member firms for ethical violations
2. **Ethical Reckoning**: Industry-wide discussion about reputation management for controversial clients and the boundaries of PR practice
3. **Precedent Set**: First expulsion of a firm as prominent as Bell Pottinger from PRCA, signaling zero tolerance for racial incitement

**For South Africa:**

1. **Political Impact**: The scandal intensified pressure on President Zuma, who faced growing calls to resign over Gupta ties
2. **Public Discourse**: The "white monopoly capital" narrative, while artificial, gained traction and became part of political discourse
3. **Media Scrutiny**: Increased investigation into state capture and Gupta family influence
4. **Civil Society**: Protests and activism against both the Guptas and Bell Pottinger's role

**For the Gupta Family:**

- Continued denial of all corruption allegations
- Oakbay Investments faced increased scrutiny and business challenges
- The scandal contributed to broader state capture investigations in South Africa

### Financial Details

- **Monthly Fee**: £100,000 from Oakbay Investments to Bell Pottinger
- **Chris Geoghegan's Fees**: £120,000 total for introducing the Guptas to Bell Pottinger
- **Herbert Smith Freehills Investigation Cost**: Reportedly £1 million
- **Bell Pottinger Liabilities (August 2017)**: £15.7 million, including £6.8m bank loan
- **Creditors Owed**: £14 million total
- **BDO Fees (by Oct 2019)**: £1.2 million
- **Repayment Demands**: £4 million from ~40 former partners; Henderson asked to repay £400,000
- **Recovered (by Oct 2019)**: 21 former partners repaid £468,000

## Open Questions

1. **Extent of Knowledge**: To what extent did Bell Pottinger's senior leadership (particularly James Henderson and Lord Bell) know about the specific tactics being employed in South Africa before they became public?

2. **Client Instructions**: What were the exact instructions from the Gupta family/Oakbay Investments to Bell Pottinger? Were they explicitly asked to stoke racial tensions, or was this a strategic interpretation by the PR firm?

3. **Twitter Bot Evidence**: The Herbert Smith Freehills investigation found no direct evidence of Bell Pottinger using Twitter bots. Who, then, was behind the hundreds of fake Twitter accounts, and what was their relationship to Bell Pottinger or the Guptas?

4. **Financial Flows**: Beyond the reported £100,000/month fee, what were the complete financial arrangements between Oakbay Investments and Bell Pottinger?

5. **Other Clients**: Did Bell Pottinger employ similar tactics for other controversial clients, and if so, which ones?

6. **Regulatory Gaps**: The scandal exposed that PR firms can face severe reputational damage but limited direct legal consequences for ethical violations. Should there be stronger statutory regulation of the PR industry?

7. **Long-term Impact**: How has the Bell Pottinger scandal affected the PR industry's approach to controversial clients and ethical boundaries?

## Recommendations / Next Steps

### For PR Professionals and Firms

1. **Enhanced Due Diligence**: Implement rigorous due diligence processes for all potential clients, particularly those with political connections or controversy
2. **Ethical Training**: Mandatory ongoing ethics training for all staff, with specific focus on racial sensitivity and disinformation risks
3. **Content Oversight**: Establish multi-layered approval processes for all client content, with legal and ethical review for potentially divisive material
4. **Whistleblower Protections**: Create robust internal mechanisms for staff to raise ethical concerns without fear of retaliation

### For Industry Bodies

1. **Stronger Enforcement**: PRCA and similar bodies should consider more proactive monitoring and earlier intervention in potential ethical violations
2. **Transparency Requirements**: Mandate greater transparency in client disclosures, particularly for foreign clients with political connections
3. **International Cooperation**: Establish cross-border cooperation mechanisms for investigating PR firms operating in multiple jurisdictions

### For Journalists and Researchers

1. **FOI Requests**: Pursue Freedom of Information requests for any regulatory documents related to the case
2. **Interview Former Staff**: Conduct interviews with former Bell Pottinger employees who were not directly involved in the Oakbay account
3. **Legal Documents**: Obtain and analyze court documents from the Insolvency Service case and any civil litigation
4. **Comparative Analysis**: Study other PR scandals to identify patterns and systemic issues in the industry

### For Policymakers

1. **Statutory Regulation**: Consider whether the PR industry requires statutory regulation in addition to self-regulation
2. **Disinformation Laws**: Evaluate whether existing laws adequately address the use of disinformation tactics by PR firms
3. **Foreign Influence**: Examine whether foreign PR firms working on behalf of foreign clients in domestic political contexts require special oversight
