## Question

How does the UK government use disinformation, and what are the documented real-world cases that illustrate these practices?

---

## Executive Summary

1. **Institutionalized Disinformation Capabilities**: The UK maintains dedicated military and intelligence units for information warfare, including the **77th Brigade** (created 2015) for psychological operations and the **MI6's Operation Mass Appeal** (late 1990s-2000s) for planting disinformation in media.
2. **Iraq War as Case Study**: The 2003 Iraq invasion was preceded by systematic disinformation, including the **September Dossier** (2002) with exaggerated claims and the **"Dodgy Dossier"** (February 2003) which was plagiarized from unattributed sources, both used to justify war.
3. **Whistleblower Confirmation**: **Katharine Gun** (GCHQ, 2003) exposed a US/UK operation to spy on UN Security Council members to pressure them into supporting the Iraq invasion, revealing covert influence operations at the highest levels.
4. **Domestic Monitoring**: Despite official denials, **Defence Secretary Ben Wallace admitted in 2022** that the 77th Brigade scours social media to "assess UK disinformation," contradicting earlier claims that the unit never targeted British citizens.
5. **Legal Framework**: The **Official Secrets Act** and **National Security Act 2023** provide broad powers to criminalize disclosures, while the government has faced criticism for using military units for domestic monitoring.
6. **Foreign Interference Context**: The **2020 Intelligence and Security Committee Russia Report** documented Russian disinformation campaigns in the UK, but also revealed gaps in the UK's own counter-disinformation capabilities and potential overreach.
7. **Historical Precedents**: The **Zinoviev Letter (1924)**—allegedly a MI5 forgery—shows a century-long pattern of state-sponsored disinformation to influence public opinion and elections.

## Findings

### 1. Institutional Mechanisms for Disinformation

#### 77th Brigade: Military Information Warfare

The **77th Brigade** is a British Army formation created in January 2015, explicitly designed for **information operations and psychological warfare**. Based at Denison Barracks in Hermitage, Berkshire, it consolidated existing capabilities including psychological warfare units, media operations units, and the Military Stabilisation and Support Group (MSSG).

**Capabilities and Operations:**

- The brigade employs personnel skilled in **video production, social media advertising, data analytics, and graphic design**—many drawn from civilian marketing and research backgrounds as reservists
- WIRED magazine's investigation revealed the unit conducts **"a warfare of storyboards and narratives, videos and social media"**, with operations including regime change efforts in Zimbabwe by discrediting the Zanu PF party
- **Domestic Controversy**: In 2022, Defence Secretary Ben Wallace admitted in Parliament that the 77th Brigade **"scours social media to assess UK disinformation"**, directly contradicting the British Army's earlier statements that the unit "do not, and have never, conducted any kind of action against British citizens"
- **COVID-19 Operations**: The brigade was deployed to **counter coronavirus misinformation**, with soldiers producing content and analyzing social media sentiment
- **Whistleblower Allegations**: A 77th Brigade whistleblower told Big Brother Watch that the unit **covertly spied on British citizens posting about COVID-19 on social media**

#### MI6: Operation Mass Appeal

**Operation Mass Appeal** was an MI6 (SIS) operation alleged to have run from the **late 1990s through the 2003 Iraq invasion**, designed to plant disinformation about Iraq's weapons of mass destruction.

**Mechanism:**

- Former UN weapons inspector **Scott Ritter** revealed that MI6 approached him in **December 1997** to provide UN documents and reports to be "worked over" and planted in media outlets worldwide
- Ritter described it as **"a psychological warfare effort"** that "served as a focal point for passing MI6 intelligence on Iraq to the media, both in the UK and around the world"
- A **senior British official** admitted to the Sunday Times in December 2003 that SIS had been "at the heart of an unnamed campaign launched in the late 1990s to spread information about Saddam's development of nerve agents and other weapons"

**Impact:**

- The operation contributed to shaping public opinion about Iraq's WMD threat, which was later used to justify the 2003 invasion
- Ritter stated the goal was to **"shake up public opinion"** using "single source data of dubious quality"

### 2. Documented Cases of UK-Sponsored Disinformation

#### Iraq WMD Dossiers (2002-2003)

The UK government published **two key dossiers** to justify the Iraq invasion, both later revealed to contain significant disinformation:

**September Dossier (24 September 2002):**

- Title: *"Iraq's Weapons of Mass Destruction: The Assessment of the British Government"*
- Claimed Iraq possessed WMD, could deploy biological weapons within **45 minutes**, and had sought uranium from Africa
- **Major General Michael Laurie** (involved in production) later wrote to the Chilcot Inquiry that **"the purpose of the dossier was precisely to make a case for war, rather than setting out the available intelligence"**
- **John Scarlett** (JIC chair) memo referenced **"the benefit of obscuring the fact that in terms of WMD Iraq is not that exceptional"**—described as a proposal to mislead the public
- The **45-minute claim** became a media sensation, with headlines like "Brits 45mins from doom" (The Sun)

**February Dossier (3 February 2003) - The "Dodgy Dossier":**

- Title: *"Iraq – Its Infrastructure of Concealment, Deception and Intimidation"*
- Released by Alastair Campbell, Blair's Director of Communications
- **Channel 4 News** and researcher **Glen Rangwala** discovered it was **heavily plagiarized** from a thesis by graduate student **Ibrahim al-Marashi** and other unattributed sources
- **Verbatim copying** including typographical errors, with **amendments to strengthen tone**: "monitoring foreign embassies" → "spying on foreign embassies", "aiding opposition groups" → "supporting terrorist organisations"
- The **Foreign Affairs Select Committee** later reported that sources should have been credited and the dossier should have been checked by ministers before release
- The committee concluded the publication was **"almost wholly counter-productive"** and served only to undermine the government's credibility

#### Katharine Gun: UN Security Council Spy Operation (2003)

**Katharine Gun**, a Mandarin Chinese translator at GCHQ, exposed a **joint US/UK operation** to spy on UN Security Council members in the lead-up to the Iraq invasion.

**The Memo:**

- On **31 January 2003**, Gun read an email from **Frank Koza** (NSA) requesting GCHQ assistance in **bugging the domestic and office communications** of six non-permanent UN Security Council members
- The operation aimed to **"dig dirt"** on these diplomats to pressure them into supporting a resolution for the Iraq invasion

**The Leak and Aftermath:**

- Gun leaked the memo, which was published by *The Observer* in **March 2003**
- She was **arrested and charged** with violating the Official Secrets Act
- **All charges were dropped** at trial in 2004, widely interpreted as an attempt to avoid revealing that Blair's government had led the UK into war on false pretences
- The case was dramatized in the 2019 film *Official Secrets*

### 3. Domestic Disinformation and Monitoring

#### 77th Brigade Domestic Operations

Despite official denials, evidence has emerged of the 77th Brigade's involvement in **domestic monitoring**:

- **2020 COVID-19 Monitoring**: The brigade **monitored social media platforms throughout 2020** and worked alongside RAF soldiers
- **Whistleblower Testimony**: A 77th Brigade whistleblower told Big Brother Watch that soldiers **covertly spied on British citizens posting about COVID-19**
- **Parliamentary Admission**: Defence Secretary **Ben Wallace** admitted in a parliamentary statement that the 77th Brigade **"scours social media to assess UK disinformation"**

### 4. Historical Precedents

#### Zinoviev Letter (1924)

- A letter allegedly from **Grigory Zinoviev** to the British Communist Party, instructing them to prepare for revolution
- Published in the **Daily Mail** four days before the **1924 general election**
- Widely believed to be a **forgery** created by **MI5** or British intelligence
- The letter **contributed to the Conservative Party's victory** and the defeat of Ramsay MacDonald's Labour government

### 5. Legal and Regulatory Framework

#### Official Secrets Act

- Used to prosecute whistleblowers, including Katharine Gun
- Prosecutions under the Act have been criticized for **suppressing public interest disclosures**

#### National Security Act 2023

- Introduces new powers to combat **foreign interference and disinformation**
- Expands the legal framework for addressing **state threats**

### 6. Foreign Influence and UK Response

#### Intelligence and Security Committee Russia Report (2020)

- Described Russian influence in the UK as **"the new normal"**
- Documented **Russian disinformation campaigns** targeting UK elections and political processes
- Criticized the UK government for **underestimating the response required** to the Russian threat
- The UK was playing **"catch-up"** in responding to disinformation threats

### Conflicts and Caveats

1. **Official Denials vs. Evidence**: The UK government has repeatedly denied that the 77th Brigade targets British citizens, yet Defence Secretary Ben Wallace's parliamentary admission and whistleblower testimonies contradict these claims.
2. **Classification Constraints**: Many details of MI6 operations, including the full scope of Operation Mass Appeal, remain classified, limiting independent verification.
3. **Whistleblower Credibility**: While Katharine Gun's case is well-documented and charges were dropped, some critics argue that her actions were illegal regardless of motive. However, the prosecution's withdrawal strongly suggests official wrongdoing.
4. **Media Complicity**: The Iraq WMD dossiers were amplified by compliant media outlets, raising questions about the role of journalism in state disinformation campaigns.

1. **Transparency**: Conduct a **public inquiry** into the 77th Brigade's domestic operations and the full scope of UK government disinformation capabilities.
