## Top finding
The Counter Disinformation Unit (now NSOIT), Rapid Response Unit, and 77th Brigade formed a cross-government network that monitored politicians, journalists, academics, and ordinary citizens, with soldiers from 77th Brigade passing collected data to the Cabinet Office as part of wider counter-disinformation efforts.

They worked together to monitor British citizens' online speech, collate tweets, conduct sentiment analysis, and flag lawful content to social media companies for suppression during the COVID-19 pandemic.

# Counter Disinformation Unit Connection: The 77th Brigade, CDU, and RRU Network

## Question

What is the Counter Disinformation Unit (CDU), and how does it connect to the UK's 77th Brigade in terms of operations, structure, collaboration, and domestic monitoring activities?

## Executive Summary

1. **CDU is a government counter-disinformation body**: The Counter Disinformation Unit (CDU) was established in 2019 within the Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport (DCMS), responding to periods of acute disinformation risk including COVID-19. Following Machinery of Government changes in February 2023, it now sits within the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology (DSIT). In 2023, it was renamed the National Security and Online Information Team (NSOIT).

2. **Direct collaboration confirmed**: Multiple official and investigative sources confirm that 77th Brigade personnel **supported the UK Government's Rapid Response Unit (RRU) in the Cabinet Office** and worked to counter COVID-19 disinformation and misinformation. Ministers have explicitly stated this collaboration.

3. **Cross-government network**: Big Brother Watch's "Ministry of Truth" investigation reveals that **CDU (DCMS), RRU (Cabinet Office), and 77th Brigade (MoD) worked together** as a coordinated network to monitor and record social media posts and press activity of politicians, academics, journalists, and ordinary citizens.

4. **Domestic monitoring of citizens**: The network monitored online speech of politicians, journalists, campaigners, and ordinary British citizens during the pandemic. Soldiers from 77th Brigade **collated tweets from British citizens about COVID-19** and passed them to the Cabinet Office as part of wider cross-government efforts.

5. **Censorship requests**: The CDU **flagged lawful speech to social media companies to be suppressed or censored**, according to leaked documents and whistleblower testimony.

6. **Legal authority and secrecy**: Despite public announcements and parliamentary discussions, the government has refused at least 19 out of 20 FOI requests about the CDU, citing exemptions for "effective conduct of public affairs" and protection of relationships with social media platforms.

7. **Monitoring of prominent critics**: The CDU specifically monitored prominent critics of the UK's COVID-19 response, including epidemiologist Carl Heneghan, tropical disease researcher Alexandre de Figueiredo, campaigner Molly Kingsley, and MP David Davis.

8. **Data collection and retention**: The CDU collects and processes personal data from publicly available sources including social media platforms. DCMS (now DSIT) is the data controller under UK GDPR. Personal data is typically retained for no more than two years, though the CDU claims it does not monitor individuals and anonymises data where possible.

## Findings

### 1. Counter Disinformation Unit (CDU): Official Structure and Mandate

**Establishment and Evolution**:
- The Counter Disinformation Unit (CDU) was **first set up within the Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport (DCMS) in 2019**
- It responded to periods of acute disinformation risk, including COVID-19
- Following Machinery of Government changes in February 2023, the unit moved to the **Department for Science, Innovation and Technology (DSIT)**
- In 2023, the CDU was **renamed the National Security and Online Information Team (NSOIT)**

**Leadership and Personnel**:
- The unit was run by civil servant **Sarah Connolly**
- **Claire Fradley** served as Head of the Counter Disinformation Unit, Security and Online Harms, DSIT (Jun 2021 - May 2023)
- NSOIT has appointed **Crisp Thinking** to help conduct analysis of social media platforms

**Origins**:
- A policy team leading on disinformation was first established in **March 2018, following the Salisbury poisonings**
- Andy Pryce, then head of counter-disinformation and media development at the FCDO, led the initial response to Russian disinformation surrounding the Salisbury incident
- This team laid the groundwork for formal structures, evolving into the CDU

**Mandate and Activities**:
- CDU leads the UK government's **operational response to disinformation threats online**
- It ensures the government takes necessary steps to **identify and respond to acute misinformation and disinformation** in areas of public interest
- Its purpose is to **understand disinformation narratives and attempts to artificially manipulate the information environment**
- CDU uses **publicly available data, including material shared on social media platforms**, to develop understanding of disinformation narratives and trends
- It does not, and has never, monitored individuals (according to official statements), and all data is anonymised wherever possible

**Data Collection and Privacy**:
- **DCMS (now DSIT) is the data controller under UK GDPR** for personal information processed by CDU
- CDU collects and processes personal data when it has not obtained it directly from individuals
- Personal data is typically retained for **no more than two years** from collection, in line with DCMS retention policy
- The CDU's privacy notice explains how it handles personal data collected from publicly available sources

**Actions and Interventions**:
- CDU responds to narratives by **rebutting social media posts, flagging posts, or promoting public health campaigns**
- Response includes **downranking posts** on social media platforms
- CDU works closely with **major social media platforms** to understand their terms of service and encourage them to promote authoritative sources of information
- For example, CDU provided links to NHS information on COVID vaccines

### 2. Rapid Response Unit (RRU): The Cabinet Office Component

**Establishment and Structure**:
- The Rapid Response Unit (RRU) was **set up in 2018**
- It was **based across the Cabinet Office and Number 10**
- RRU was designed to **spot "stories of concern"** in order to counter "a range of harmful narratives online"
- According to the government, the RRU has since been **disbanded** (as of 2023)

**Activities**:
- RRU shared media coverage with other teams in government to update on how policies and issues were being reported on
- CDU received these updates along with many other teams
- RRU pressured a Whitehall department to attack newspapers for publishing articles analysing COVID-19 modelling that it feared would "affect compliance" with pandemic restrictions
- RRU staff featured Conservative MPs, activists, and journalists in "vaccine hesitancy reports" for opposing vaccine passports

**Connection to 77th Brigade**:
- **General Sir Nick Carter**, Chief of the Defence Staff, confirmed that **77th Brigade was "helping to quash rumours about misinformation, but also to counter disinformation"**
- **Armed forces minister James Heappey** confirmed that 77th Brigade was playing a **key role in Whitehall's battle against coronavirus**
- Heappey stated: "Among other things, the Brigade was said to be **'supporting the government's Rapid Response Unit in the Cabinet Office'**—a body set up in 2018 'to identify and rebut disinformation and misinformation', according to the government"
- **Ministers have previously said** that **members of the 77th Brigade supported the UK Government's Rapid Response Unit (RRU) in the Cabinet Office** and were working to counter COVID-19 disinformation and misinformation

### 3. The Coordinated Network: CDU, RRU, and 77th Brigade

**Big Brother Watch's "Ministry of Truth" Investigation**:
- Big Brother Watch published a comprehensive report on **29 January 2023** titled "Ministry of Truth: the secretive government units spying on your speech"
- The report contains **a huge cache of documents** obtained via FOI requests relating to 77th Brigade and RAF intelligence support for Whitehall's "Ministry of Truth"
- The investigation includes **evidence from a 77th Brigade whistleblower** that, despite claims to the contrary by senior generals, **troops did spy on the British public**

**Direct Collaboration Evidence**:
- **Documents obtained by Big Brother Watch exposed the work of government cells** including the **Counter Disinformation Unit (based in DCMS)** and the **Rapid Response Unit (in the Cabinet Office)**
- **Soldiers from the Army's 77th Brigade collated tweets from British citizens about Covid-19 at the start of the pandemic** and passed them to the Cabinet Office as part of wider cross-Government efforts to combat misinformation and disinformation about Covid-19
- **Troops also conducted "sentiment analysis"** about the government's Covid-19 response
- Big Brother Watch's "Ministry of Truth" report alleges that **government units including CDU (DCMS), RRU (Cabinet Office) and 77th Brigade worked together** to monitor and record the social media posts and press activity of politicians, academics and journalists

**Monitoring of Prominent Figures**:
- The CDU specifically **monitored prominent critics of the United Kingdom's responses to the COVID-19 pandemic**
- Monitored individuals included:
  - **Epidemiologist Carl Heneghan**
  - **Tropical disease researcher Alexandre de Figueiredo**
  - **Molly Kingsley**, who ran a campaign to keep schools open during the pandemic
  - **Member of Parliament David Davis**, who had his comments logged by the unit
- **Conservative MP and former Home Secretary David Davis** was among those cited in CDU files as "critical of the Government" after questioning the mathematical reasoning behind Imperial University's pandemic modelling

**Censorship Activities**:
- The **CDU flagged lawful speech to social media companies to be suppressed or censored**
- The government had set up secretive units (CDU and RRU) that **monitored the online speech of politicians, journalists, campaigners, and ordinary British citizens during the pandemic**
- The Daily Telegraph reported that **Twitter refused majority of removal requests from Covid spying unit**

### 4. Parliamentary Oversight and Government Transparency

**Government Fact Sheet Claims**:
- The government claims the **CDU's existence was publicly announced in a press release**
- The then Culture Secretary **discussed the workings of the unit with Parliament in an April 2020 Select Committee evidence session**
- It has been **talked about more than 200 times in Parliament** and has been scrutinised by the DCMS Select Committee with public hearings
- The government states it **welcomes this scrutiny from MPs on the CDU's work**

**Reality of Transparency**:
- **Big Brother Watch has submitted at least 20 FOI requests to DCMS and DSIT about the CDU**
- **19 of these were rejected in full** and one received a response containing heavily redacted minutes
- Questions have ranged from simple staffing and budget levels to requests for reports commissioned by the CDU and emails it has exchanged with social media companies
- **The CDU's existence may not be a secret, but its department refuses point blank to publish any information** asked for via FOI requests or by MPs in the House of Commons
- Any claim that DSIT "welcomes" scrutiny of the CDU is **highly questionable** as it refuses to be subjected to any

**FOI Exemptions and Delays**:
- Government withholds information on CDU citing FOI exemptions where **"disclosure would, or would be likely to, prejudice the effective conduct of public affairs"**
- To apply exemptions, DCMS sought the "reasonable opinion" of a **minister of the crown** (unnamed)
- **PublicTechnology.net** asked basic questions: how many FTE staff worked at the unit, how many pieces of disinformation identified since March 2020, outline details of three most recent instances of flagged/removed information
- **DCMS took 126 working days** to respond to PublicTechnology's FOI request (regulations require response within 20 working days)
- Secrecy is maintained on grounds of protecting government's **"relationship with social media platforms"** and preserving a **"safe space" around ministers and government officials**

**Parliamentary Oversight**:
- The **DSIT Select Committee** (formerly DCMS Select Committee) **keeps the unit's performance under constant review**, as with any group or team in government
- The **DSIT Secretary of State is accountable to Parliament** for the CDU's work
- In a House of Lords debate (18 July 2023), it was confirmed that CDU is **subject to parliamentary oversight**

**Legal and Constitutional Concerns**:
- **Article 10 of the ECHR** sets out that member states' interferences with the right to freedom of expression should be provided by law
- **The CDU and other anti-disinformation units aren't authorised by an act of parliament** and currently have **no formal judicial or law enforcement function**
- This raises serious constitutional questions about the legal basis for these monitoring activities

### 6. Connection to 77th Brigade Operations

**Direct Support**:
- **Members of 77th Brigade supported the UK Government's Rapid Response Unit (RRU) in the Cabinet Office**
- They were **working to counter Covid-19 disinformation and misinformation**

**Data Collection**:
- **Soldiers from the Army's 77th Brigade collated tweets from British citizens about Covid-19** at the start of the pandemic
- These were **passed to the Cabinet Office** as part of wider cross-Government efforts
- **Troops conducted "sentiment analysis"** about the government's Covid-19 response

**Whistleblower Evidence**:
- A **77th Brigade whistleblower** revealed that the Brigade was **"monitoring the UK population"** to gauge public response to government policies
- This often included reports on **British people sharing their fears around COVID-19**
- The whistleblower contradicted senior generals' claims that troops had never conducted actions against British citizens

**Defence Cultural Specialist Unit (DCSU) Role**:
- The **Defence Cultural Specialist Unit within 77th Brigade was specifically tasked with monitoring internet content related to COVID-19 vaccines**
- This represents **direct military monitoring of domestic online content**

**Government Admissions**:
- Defence Secretary Ben Wallace admitted in Parliament that **77th Brigade's counter-disinformation capabilities have been used to assess UK disinformation trends**
- He stated he had **instructed an investigation** into whistleblower allegations

### 7. Legal Framework and Data Protection

**Data Controller**:
- **DCMS (now DSIT) is the data controller under UK GDPR** for personal information processed by CDU
- CDU's privacy notice explains data collection from publicly available sources

**Retention Policy**:
- Personal data typically retained for **no more than two years** from collection
- May be kept longer if required by law (e.g., for public inquiries)

**Official Denials vs. Evidence**:
- Government claims CDU **does not, and has never, monitored individuals**
- CDU claims **all data is anonymised wherever possible**
- However, evidence shows **specific individuals were monitored** (Heneghan, de Figueiredo, Kingsley, Davis)
- **77th Brigade collected identifiable tweets from British citizens**

**FOI Refusals**:
- Government refuses to disclose:
  - Staffing levels and budget
  - Reports commissioned by CDU
  - Emails exchanged with social media companies
  - Specific instances of flagged or removed content
- Justifications include protecting **relationship with social media platforms** and **safe space for ministers**
