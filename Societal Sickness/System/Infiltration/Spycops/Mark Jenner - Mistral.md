## Findings

### 1. The 2011 High Court Case - Eight Women vs. Metropolitan Police

**Case Details**
- **Filed**: December 2011
- **Claimants**: Eight women deceived into long-term intimate relationships with undercover officers
- **Defendants**: Metropolitan Police Service and Association of Chief Police Officers (ACPO)
- **Officers Involved**: Mark Kennedy, Bob Lambert, Jim Boyling, Mark Jenner (as Mark Cassidy), John Dines
- **Legal Claims**: Deceit, assault, misfeasance in public office, negligence, human rights violations (Articles 3, 8, 12 of ECHR)

**Key Developments**
- The women sought to highlight and prevent psychological, emotional, and sexual abuse by undercover officers
- Police attempted to have the case struck out, sent to secret court, and hid behind NCND policy
- In 2013, Jenner's photograph, cover name, and real name were published in the press
- Alison (pseudonym) gave testimony to the Home Affairs Select Committee about her five-year relationship with Jenner

**Significance for Jenner**
- Alison was one of the eight claimants; she lived with Mark Cassidy for five years (1995-2000)
- Jenner was specifically cited in the legal case brought against the Metropolitan Police
- The case established that Jenner had been married with children throughout his deployment and relationship with Alison

### 2. The 2014 NCND Legal Battle

**Case**: Women vs. Metropolitan Police (NCND Challenge)
- **Date**: August 14, 2014
- **Judge**: Mr. Justice Bean
- **Court**: High Court of Justice

**Ruling**
- Police could no longer rely on NCND policy for Bob Lambert and Jim Boyling (already publicly confirmed)
- **For Mark Jenner and John Dines**: Despite overwhelming evidence, the judge ruled he could not force the naming of officers who had not outed themselves
- The court found that the police's NCND policy was being used to avoid accountability

**Impact**
- Partial victory for the women, but Jenner's identity remained officially unconfirmed
- The ruling noted that evidence against Jenner was overwhelming and confirmation was only a matter of time
- Police continued to refuse official confirmation of Jenner's identity as Mark Cassidy

### 3. The 2015 Settlement and Apology

**Settlement Details**
- **Date**: November 20, 2015
- **Parties**: Metropolitan Police settled with seven of the eight women
- **Apology**: Unprecedented public statement acknowledging wrongdoing
- **Compensation**: Estimated £3 million total for the seven claimants

**Metropolitan Police Statement (Excerpts)**
> some officers, acting undercover whilst seeking to infiltrate protest groups, entered into long-term intimate sexual relationships with women which were abusive, deceitful, manipulative and wrong.

> None of the women with whom the undercover officers had a relationship brought it on themselves. They were deceived pure and simple.

> It was a gross violation and also accept that it may well have reflected attitudes towards women that should have no part in the culture of the Metropolitan Police.

**Significance**
- First official acknowledgment that undercover relationships constituted human rights abuses
- Police accepted that supervising officers knew about and failed to act on the relationships
- **Jenner's Status**: Still not officially confirmed by police, despite being publicly identified since 2011

### 4. The 2018 Mitting Ruling on Cover Names

**Case**: Undercover Policing Inquiry - Restriction Order Applications
- **Date**: April 11, 2018
- **Judge**: Sir John Mitting (Inquiry Chair)

**Ruling on Mark Jenner (HN15)**
- Mitting rejected applications to restrict both Jenner's cover name (Mark Cassidy) and real name (Mark Jenner)
- **Key Statement**: if Jenner's name had not already been in the public domain and known to have engaged in a relationship, he would have kept both cover and real name secret
- The ruling noted that the Metropolitan Police had admitted the relationship with Alison
- Mitting stated: real name will be published where it appears in the evidence in due course

**Implications**
- Confirmed that Jenner's identity was already public knowledge
- Established that his cover name and real name would appear in inquiry evidence
- Maintained that if not for prior publicity, both names would have been kept secret

### 5. Undercover Policing Inquiry (UCPI) - Mark Jenners Testimony

**Inquiry Details**
- **Announced**: March 2015 by Home Secretary Theresa May
- **Chair**: Sir John Mitting (since July 2017)
- **Scope**: Undercover policing in England and Wales since 1968
- **Jenner's Status**: One of 139 officers under investigation

**Jenner's Deployment**
- **Cover Name**: Mark Cassidy
- **Real Name**: Mark Jenner
- **Codename**: Touchy Subject (revealed in inquiry)
- **Deployment Period**: 1995-2000 (most active 1995-1999)
- **Unit**: Special Demonstration Squad (SDS)
- **Targets**: Colin Roach Centre, Anti-Fascist Action, Independent Working Class Association, Republican Forum, Red Action
- **Groups Infiltrated**: Construction union UCATT, Building Worker Safety Campaign, RMT, Unison, CPSA, TGWU

**Jenner's Testimony (2024-2026)**
- **February 2026**: Jenner began giving testimony to the inquiry
- **Admission**: Acknowledged deceiving women into relationships
- **Controversial Statement**: Claimed sexual relations were necessary for undercover work
- **Justification**: Stated sexual gratification was definitely not a perk of the job
- **Personal Life**: Maintained a double life - spending days with his wife and evenings with Alison, each believing he was at work
- **Travel**: Requests for travel to pursue target groups were actually holidays abroad with Alison

**Alisons Response**
- Testified that Jenner's claim about her landline phone being his motivation for the relationship was complete nonsense and ridiculous
- Stated that any illusions, or romantic memories of him being an anti-fascist, anti-racist, trade union activist were demolished after hearing his evidence
- Believes his direct managers knew and covered up the deception

**Inquiry Context**
- Jenner is one of 139 undercover officers being investigated
- Inquiry has confirmed infiltration of over 1,000 political groups
- Expected to become the most expensive British public inquiry (projected £200M+)
- Criticized for delays and allowing officers to remain anonymous

**Police Secrecy**

- Despite public identification since January 2011
- Police continue to protect the identities of abusers
