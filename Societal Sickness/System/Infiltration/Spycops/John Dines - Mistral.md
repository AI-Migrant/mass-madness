# John Dines Cases: A Deep Dive into the Legal and Institutional Battles

## Question
What are the specific legal cases, court battles, public inquiries, and institutional responses involving former undercover police officer John Dines, focusing on his deployment in political activist groups, the deception of women into intimate relationships, and the subsequent accountability mechanisms?

## Executive Summary

John Dines, operating under the alias **"John Barker"**, was a Special Demonstration Squad (SDS) officer who infiltrated London Greenpeace and other activist groups from **1987-1992**. His case is central to the UK's **Spycops scandal**, marked by three major legal and institutional battles:

1. **The Relationship Deception Litigation (2011-2015)**: Eight women, including Helen Steel, sued the Metropolitan Police for being deceived into long-term intimate relationships. The case settled in **November 2015** with an unprecedented apology and financial compensation (estimated £3M total), where police admitted the relationships were **"abusive, deceitful, manipulative and wrong"** and violated the women's human rights.

2. **The McLibel Connection**: Dines' infiltration intersected with the historic **McLibel case** (1990-2005). Helen Steel was served the libel writ as she stepped from Dines' van in 1990. The Met later settled (2000, £10,000) with Steel and Dave Morris for unlawfully passing intelligence to McDonald's private investigators.

3. **Undercover Policing Inquiry (2014-present)**: Dines was officially named as an undercover officer in **December 2016** (HN5). He refused to testify in person but submitted a written statement. Helen Steel gave evidence in **2024-2025**. The Inquiry confirmed Dines stole the identity of **John Barker, an 8-year-old child who died of leukaemia**.

4. **Australia Controversy**: After exposure, Dines relocated to Australia, where he worked at the **Australian Graduate School of Policing & Security**, directing training programmes for Indian police officers that included targeting **left-wing extremist groups**.

**Key Finding**: Despite settlements and apologies, the Metropolitan Police maintained their **"Neither Confirm Nor Deny" (NCND)** policy regarding Dines' identity until the Inquiry forced disclosure in 2016.

## Findings

### 1. The Deployment: Infiltration of London Greenpeace (1987-1992)

**Unit**: Special Demonstration Squad (SDS), Metropolitan Police
**Cover Identity**: "John Barker" – **stolen identity of John Barker, an 8-year-old boy who died of leukaemia**
**Target Groups**: Primarily London Greenpeace; also involved with other North London activist campaigns
**Timeline**: 
- **1987**: Dines deployed as "John Barker"
- **Late 1980s**: Met Helen Steel at London Greenpeace
- **1990-1992**: Two-year intimate relationship with Steel; they lived together and discussed starting a family
- **Early 1992**: Dines abruptly disappeared, claiming a breakdown

**Role in London Greenpeace**:
- Served as **treasurer** of the group
- Present during early McLibel case hearings
- Collected intelligence on campaigners

> **Source Note**: Helen Steel's testimony and campaign group archives confirm Dines' central role in London Greenpeace during the period leading up to the McLibel trial. The identity theft was confirmed through Steel's 19-year search, which revealed the use of a deceased child's birth certificate.

---

### 2. The McLibel Intersection: Corporate-State Collaboration

**Background**: The McLibel case (1990-2005) was a libel action brought by McDonald's Corporation against London Greenpeace activists for distributing a critical pamphlet. It became the longest trial in English legal history.

**Dines' Connection**:
- **September 1990**: Helen Steel was served with the libel writ **as she stepped from John Dines' van** outside his bedsit in Burgoyne Road
- **Intelligence sharing**: McDonald's private investigators received information from the Metropolitan Police
- **2000 Settlement**: Steel and Dave Morris sued the Met for unlawfully passing intelligence to McDonald's. The Met **apologized and paid £10,000** in an out-of-court settlement, with an undertaking not to share police computer information with corporations

**Significance**: This case reveals the **collaboration between corporate private investigators and undercover police** in monitoring activist groups, with Dines' presence at a critical moment in the legal process.

> **Source Note**: Wikipedia entries on Helen Steel and London Greenpeace, and campaign archives, document this intersection. The 2000 settlement is a separate legal action from the 2015 relationship deception case.

---

### 3. The Relationship Deception Litigation (2011-2015)

**Case Overview**:
- **Filed**: December 2011
- **Claimants**: Eight women (including Helen Steel) deceived into long-term relationships by five undercover officers (Dines, Bob Lambert, Jim Boyling, Mark Kennedy, Mark Jenner)
- **Defendants**: Metropolitan Police Service and Association of Chief Police Officers (ACPO)
- **Legal claims**: Deceit, assault, misfeasance in public office, negligence, human rights violations (Articles 3, 8, 12 ECHR)

**Settlement**:
- **Date**: 20 November 2015
- **Outcome**: Out-of-court settlement for **seven of eight claimants**
- **Apology**: **Unreserved public apology** from Assistant Commissioner Martin Hewitt on behalf of the Met
- **Financial**: Estimated **£3 million total** compensation for the seven settled cases
- **Admission**: Police conceded the relationships were **"abusive, deceitful, manipulative and wrong"** and constituted **"a gross violation" of the women's human rights** with "severely harmful effects"

**Key Quotes from the Apology**:
> "I unreservedly apologise on behalf of the Metropolitan Police Service. I am aware that money alone cannot compensate the loss of time, their hurt or the feelings of abuse caused by these relationships."
> "None of the women with whom the undercover officers had a relationship brought it on themselves. They were deceived pure and simple."
> "The Metropolitan Police recognizes that this should never happen again and the necessary steps must be taken to ensure that it does not."

**The NCND Problem**: Despite the settlement and apology, the Met **continued to refuse to confirm Dines' identity** as a police officer, citing their **"Neither Confirm Nor Deny" (NCND)** policy. This only changed when the Undercover Policing Inquiry named him in December 2016.

> **Source Note**: The full apology text is available on the Police Spies Out of Lives website. The settlement represented the first time the Met admitted to the relationships and their abusive nature.

### 4. The Identity Theft Revelation

**Discovery Timeline**:
- **1994**: Helen Steel discovered "John Barker" had used the identity of a child who died young
- **2003**: Learned he had been a married police officer
- **Late 2010**: Confirmed Dines had been an undercover officer
- **December 2016**: Undercover Policing Inquiry officially named John Dines as an undercover police officer (HN5)

**The Stolen Identity**:
- **Real name**: John Barker
- **Status**: Deceased child
- **Cause of death**: Leukaemia
- **Age at death**: 8 years old

**Pattern**: This was part of a **systematic SDS practice** of using dead children's identities to create undercover personas, later exposed as a widespread abuse within the unit.

> **Source Note**: Confirmed by Helen Steel's testimony, campaign archives, and the UCPI. The practice of using dead children's identities was a key revelation of the Spycops scandal.

### 5. The Undercover Policing Inquiry (UCPI)

**Establishment**: Announced by Home Secretary Theresa May on **6 March 2014**, following revelations about undercover policing abuses.

**Dines' Status**:
- **Codenames**: HN5 (SDS officer number)
- **Official confirmation**: Named as undercover officer in **December 2016**
- **Participation**: **Refused to appear as a witness**; submitted written statement only
- **Helen Steel's evidence**: Gave testimony in **2024 and 2025**

**Inquiry Scope Relevant to Dines**:
- Investigation of SDS operations (1968-2008)
- Examination of **1,000+ groups** infiltrated, including London Greenpeace
- Focus on **intimate relationships as institutional abuse**
- Scrutiny of **identity theft practices**

**Current Status**:
- Inquiry chair: Sir John Mitting (since July 2017)
- **Interim Report (Tranche One)**: Published 29 June 2023
- **Cost to date**: £102 million (inquiry) + £68.6 million (policing costs) as of 2025
- **Projected completion**: Extended to potentially 2032

> **Source Note**: UCPI website, Wikipedia, and campaign group reports. The Inquiry has faced criticism for delays and allowing officers to remain anonymous.

### 6. The Australia Controversy (2016-Present)

**Relocation**: After his identity was exposed, Dines was **moved to Australia by the Metropolitan Police** to prevent Helen Steel from finding him (according to a police source quoted in The Guardian).

**Employment**:
- **Institution**: Australian Graduate School of Policing & Security
- **Role**: Course director
- **Programme**: Training programme for **Indian Police officers**
- **Content**: Included training on **"emerging challenges, viz, Left Wing Extremist and other low-intensity conflicts"**

**Confrontation**:
- **February 2016**: Helen Steel discovered Dines' location in Australia
- **2016**: Steel confronted Dines in Sydney; he **apologized to her**

**Public Outcry**: Campaign groups and politicians demanded Dines cease teaching police tactics, given his history of human rights abuses. A **statement was made in the Australian Parliament** demanding his removal from police training roles.

> **Source Note**: Campaign Opposing Police Surveillance archives, Vice investigation, and Police Spies Out of Lives statements document Dines' Australian employment and the ethical concerns raised.

### 7. Broader Context: The Spycops Scandal

**SDS Practices Exposed**:
- **Systematic deception**: Over 140 undercover officers deployed over 40+ years
- **Target groups**: 1,000+ political groups, primarily left-wing, environmental, and social justice organizations
- **Relationship abuse**: At least **18 women** (known) deceived into relationships; some officers fathered children with activists
- **Identity theft**: **80+ confirmed cases** of officers using dead children's identities

**Other Officers in the Litigation**:
- **Bob Lambert** (HN10): Preceded Dines in London Greenpeace; co-wrote the McLibel pamphlet; had relationship with another activist
- **Jim Boyling** (HN12): Relationship with another claimant
- **Mark Kennedy** (HN??): Multiple relationships; later exposed as undercover
- **Mark Jenner**: Relationship with "Alison"; identity still not officially confirmed by police

**Institutional Response**:
- **2014**: Public inquiry announced
- **2015**: Met apology and settlement for relationship cases
- **2016**: First officers officially named by Inquiry
- **2020s**: Ongoing evidence hearings; delayed by police legal tactics
