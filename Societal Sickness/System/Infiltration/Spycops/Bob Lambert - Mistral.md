Bob Lambert, Spycops, UK, case, undercover

The deep dive into Bob Lambert's Spycops cases covers his undercover deployment (1983-1988) as "Bob Robinson" in London Greenpeace, co-authoring the McLibel leaflet, sexual relationships with four women (including fathering a child and a £425,000 settlement), arson allegations from the 1987 Debenhams firebombing, his later management of the SDS, founding the Muslim Contact Unit, and ongoing Undercover Policing Inquiry proceedings.

### Top finding
Lambert's most damaging legacy is the McLibel case connection—he co-wrote the leaflet that triggered the longest trial in English history while the court was never told a police spy was involved, compounded by human rights violations through deceptive relationships and potential criminal conduct.

# Bob Lambert: Deep Dive into Spycops Cases

## Question

What were the specific cases, controversies, and legal proceedings involving former undercover police officer Bob Lambert during his deployment in the Special Demonstration Squad (SDS) and subsequent career?

## Executive Summary

1. **McLibel Scandal**: Lambert co-authored the 1986 London Greenpeace leaflet *"What's Wrong with McDonald's?"* under his alias "Bob Robinson", which became the centerpiece of the historic McLibel libel trial (1990-1997) – the longest in English legal history. The court was never informed of his undercover status.
2. **Sexual Relationships &amp; Human Rights Violations**: While undercover (1983-1988), Lambert formed long-term intimate relationships with at least four women under false pretenses, fathering a child with one activist (Jacqui) in 1985. The Metropolitan Police paid £425,000 in damages in 2014 after Jacqui and her son required psychiatric treatment upon discovering his true identity.
3. **Arson Allegations**: Accused by jailed Animal Liberation Front member Geoff Sheppard and MP Caroline Lucas of planting an incendiary device at Debenhams Harrow in 1987 to bolster his credibility as an activist. Lambert denies the allegation, but his evidence helped convict two men for the firebombings.
4. **SDS Management Role**: After his deployment, Lambert became a manager of the SDS in the 1990s, overseeing some of the unit's most controversial excesses. He contributed to the SDS *Tradecraft Manual* and authored the 1997 *Withdrawal Strategy* document.
5. **Muslim Contact Unit**: Founded and led the Metropolitan Police's Muslim Contact Unit (MCU) from 2002-2007, established to build partnerships with Muslim communities post-9/11.
6. **Academic Career &amp; Exposure**: After retiring in 2007, Lambert became an academic at St Andrews, London Metropolitan University, and Exeter. He was forced to resign from his positions in December 2015 after his undercover past was exposed.
7. **Ongoing Inquiry**: The Undercover Policing Inquiry is examining Lambert's role in the McLibel case, his undercover activities, and his subsequent management of the SDS. He is expected to return to give evidence about his managerial period.

## Methodology

This research synthesizes public records, investigative journalism, court documents, and inquiry materials. Primary sources include:

- Undercover Policing Inquiry transcripts and primers
- Investigative books (*Undercover: The True Story of Britain's Secret Police* by Evans &amp; Lewis)
- BBC, Guardian, and Independent investigative reports
- Parliamentary statements and legal settlements
- Academic and activist research (Powerbase, Campaign Opposing Police Surveillance)

Search angles focused on: Lambert's specific deployments, legal cases (McLibel, settlements), criminal allegations (arson), managerial role in SDS, MCU establishment, and recent inquiry developments.

## Findings

### 1. Undercover Deployment (1983-1988)

**Alias**: "Bob Robinson"  
**Target Groups**: London Greenpeace, Animal Liberation Front, environmental and animal rights activists  
**Duration**: Approximately 5 years (early 1984 to late 1988)

Lambert infiltrated London Greenpeace using the stolen identity of a dead child. He established deep cover by forming genuine friendships and relationships, becoming a trusted member of the activist community. His deployment was considered the "gold standard" for undercover policing, earning him commendations.

**Key Activities**:

- Co-authored the *"What's Wrong with McDonald's?"* factsheet in 1986
- Participated in street distributions of the leaflet outside McDonald's restaurants
- Attended book fairs and campaign events as an activist
- Allegedly engaged in more clandestine activities to maintain credibility

### 2. The McLibel Case Connection

**Leaflet Creation (1986)**: Lambert was one of a small group (4-5 people) who co-wrote the six-page leaflet *"What's Wrong with McDonald's: everything they don't want you to know"*. The leaflet made allegations about McDonald's labor practices, environmental damage, animal cruelty, and health impacts.

**Legal Proceedings (1990-1997)**: McDonald's sued five London Greenpeace activists for libel. Three settled; Helen Steel and David Morris (the "McLibel Two") fought the case. The trial became the longest in English history (313 days).

**Court Deception**: The court was never informed that one of the leaflet's authors was an undercover police officer. Lambert's involvement was hidden throughout the proceedings, representing a violation of legal professional privilege.

**Outcome**: Steel and Morris lost the case in 1997 and were ordered to pay £60,000, but McDonald's chose not to enforce the judgment. The European Court of Human Rights later ruled (2005) that the pair had been denied a fair trial, violating Article 6 (right to fair trial) and Article 10 (freedom of expression).

**Inquiry Focus**: The Undercover Policing Inquiry is examining whether Lambert's role in creating the leaflet and his subsequent court appearances (as both defendant and witness) constituted an abuse of the judicial system.

### 3. Sexual Relationships &amp; Human Rights Abuses

**Relationships**: Lambert had long-term intimate relationships with at least four women while undercover:

1. **Jacqui**: A 22-year-old activist when they met. Lambert fathered her son in 1985. When the child was two, Lambert (as "Bob Robinson") vanished. Jacqui only discovered his true identity in 2012, 24 years later.
2. **Belinda Harvey**: A non-politically active woman. Lambert's Special Branch colleagues raided her home to bolster his cover story as a "hardcore militant."
3. **Two additional women**: Identities not publicly disclosed.

**Legal Settlement**: On 23 October 2014, the Metropolitan Police agreed to pay £425,000 to Jacqui as part of an agreement to drop her legal action alleging assault, negligence, deceit, and misconduct by senior officers. Both Jacqui and her son required psychiatric treatment after learning Lambert's true identity.

**Lambert's Response**: In July 2013, Lambert acknowledged conducting "four such relationships under false pretences" and stated he had "made serious mistakes that I should regret, and I always will do." He apologized to the women and to London Greenpeace members.

**Systemic Issue**: This was part of a broader pattern – the Met eventually apologized and paid compensation to eight women who had relationships with undercover officers, admitting the relationships were "abusive, deceitful, manipulative and wrong." However, the police maintained there was never a formal policy authorizing such behavior.

### 4. Arson Allegations (1987)

**Debenhams Firebombings**: On an unspecified date in 1987, three Debenhams department stores were firebombed in Harrow, Romford, and Luton, causing £8 million in damage. The attacks were part of an Animal Liberation Front campaign against fur retailers.

**Accusation**: In June 2012, Green MP Caroline Lucas used parliamentary privilege to reveal that jailed ALF member Geoff Sheppard believed Lambert planted the incendiary device at the Harrow store. Sheppard stated: "I straight away knew that Bob had carried out his part of the plan."

**Lambert's Role**: Lambert infiltrated the ALF and his evidence helped convict two men (Geoff Sheppard and another) of the firebombings. Sheppard served several years in prison.

**Lambert's Denial**: Lambert has consistently denied planting the device, stating in a Guardian interview that he did not participate in the attack.

**Cover Story**: Lambert used the pretext of being under police investigation for the Debenhams arson to explain his need to go "on the run" in late 1988, which was actually his extraction from undercover deployment. Special Branch even raided Belinda Harvey's home to add credibility to this cover story.

**Motivation Allegation**: Critics suggest Lambert may have planted the device to prove his worth to the ALF and bolster his activist credibility.

### 5. SDS Management &amp; Oversight (1990s)

**Promotion**: After his undercover deployment ended, Lambert was promoted to a management position within the SDS.

**Oversight**: He oversaw some of the "worst excesses" of the SDS during the 1990s, according to the Campaign Opposing Police Surveillance.

**Tradecraft Manual**: Lambert contributed significantly to the SDS *Tradecraft Manual*, described as "an organic document of initiatives, operational learning, guidelines and suggestions from established UCOs \[undercover officers\] to assist other UCOs in their deployments."

**Withdrawal Strategy**: In 1997, Lambert authored a confidential Special Branch document called *Withdrawal Strategy*, which discussed Peter Francis' exfiltration from his undercover deployment.

**Mike Chitty Investigation**: Around June 1992, while still in E Squad, Lambert was tasked by Special Branch superiors to "discreetly study the rogue officer" Mike Chitty, a contemporary SDS undercover officer who had infiltrated animal rights activists in the mid-1980s.

### 6. Muslim Contact Unit (2002-2007)

**Establishment**: In January 2002, Lambert founded the Muslim Contact Unit (MCU) within the Metropolitan Police Special Branch, conceived in the aftermath of the 9/11 attacks.

**Mission**: The MCU's primary mission was "establishing partnerships with Muslim community leaders both equipped and located to help tackle the spread of al-Qaida propaganda in London."

**Leadership**: Lambert led the MCU through the 2006 merger of MPSB (SO12) with the Anti-Terrorist Branch (SO13), which created the Counter Terrorism Command (SO15). He retired in December 2007.

**Controversy**: The MCU's approach of working closely with Islamist groups like the Muslim Association of Britain (MAB) and Muslim Welfare House (MWH) has been criticized by some as potentially legitimizing extremist elements.

### 7. Academic Career &amp; Exposure (2008-2015)

**Positions**:

- Lecturer in Terrorism Studies at University of St Andrews
- Senior lecturer at London Metropolitan University's John Grieve Centre for Policing
- Co-director of the European Muslim Research Centre at Exeter University (until August 2011)

**Exposure**: Lambert was publicly exposed as a former undercover officer in October 2011 when London Greenpeace activists confronted him at a conference. Details of his past continued to emerge through 2012-2013.

**Resignation**: Under pressure from activists and the public, Lambert resigned from his academic positions in December 2015.

**Honors**: He was appointed Member of the Order of the British Empire (MBE) in the 2008 Birthday Honours for "services to the police."

### 8. Undercover Policing Inquiry (2014-Present)

**Establishment**: The public inquiry was announced by Home Secretary Theresa May in March 2014, following revelations about undercover policing abuses, including the spying on the Stephen Lawrence family.

**Lambert's Status**: Lambert is a Core Participant in the inquiry. He has given evidence about his undercover period and is expected to return to testify about his subsequent career and management of the SDS.

**Key Lines of Questioning**:

- His role in writing the McLibel leaflet
- His activities in the wider McDonald's campaign
- His court appearances (as defendant and witness)
- Potential violations of legal professional privilege
- His managerial decisions and oversight of SDS operations

**Current Status**: As of December 2024, the inquiry is ongoing. Lambert's first round of testimony focused on his undercover deployment; a second round is expected to cover his managerial period.

**Conflicts and Caveats**:

- Lambert denies the arson allegations; the accusation comes from a convicted activist (Geoff Sheppard) and has not been proven in court
- The exact number of relationships is disputed; Lambert acknowledges four, but other sources suggest there may be more victims
- Some details of Lambert's managerial period in the SDS remain classified or undisclosed
- The Undercover Policing Inquiry is still ongoing, and further evidence may emerge

## Open Questions

1. **Arson Allegations**: Will the Undercover Policing Inquiry or any criminal investigation definitively resolve whether Lambert participated in the 1987 Debenhams firebombing?
2. **Full Scope of Relationships**: How many women were deceived into relationships with Lambert, and were there similar cases involving other SDS officers that have not yet been disclosed?
3. **Managerial Responsibility**: To what extent was Lambert aware of and complicit in the "worst excesses" of the SDS during his managerial period in the 1990s?
4. **Legal Professional Privilege**: Did Lambert's involvement in the McLibel case constitute a violation of legal professional privilege, and what are the implications for other cases where undercover officers provided evidence?
5. **MCU Legacy**: What was the long-term impact of Lambert's Muslim Contact Unit on community policing and counter-terrorism strategies?
6. **Accountability**: Will Lambert or his superiors face any criminal charges or professional sanctions for the human rights violations committed during his undercover deployment?

## Recommendations / Next Steps

1. **Monitor the Undercover Policing Inquiry**: Follow Lambert's upcoming testimony about his managerial period in the SDS for new revelations about unit practices and oversight.
2. **Legal Review**: Assess whether the McLibel case and other cases involving Lambert's evidence should be reconsidered in light of his undercover status and potential violations of legal professional privilege.
3. **Victim Support**: Ensure that all women deceived into relationships with Lambert and other undercover officers have access to legal recourse and psychological support.
4. **Policy Reform**: Review Metropolitan Police policies and training to prevent the recurrence of undercover officers engaging in sexual relationships with targets.
5. **Historical Investigation**: Support independent historical research into the SDS and NPOIU to document the full extent of undercover policing abuses.
6. **Transparency**: Advocate for greater transparency in the Undercover Policing Inquiry, including the release of relevant documents and the lifting of anonymity restrictions where possible.
