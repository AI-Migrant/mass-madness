# Liz Truss's Policies and Removal: A Deep Dive

## Executive Summary

Liz Truss served as the UK Prime Minister for **49 days** (6 September 2022 – 25 October 2022), making her the shortest-serving prime minister in British history. Her tenure was marked by **radical economic policies**, a **failed mini-budget**, and a **rapid loss of confidence** from her party, the public, and financial markets. While the official reason for her resignation was the economic fallout from her policies, there are **competing narratives**—including her own claims of a "deep state" conspiracy—that suggest more complex forces at play.

### Key Takeaways

1. **Economic Policies (Trussonomics):** Truss advocated for **supply-side economics**, featuring **unfunded tax cuts**, deregulation, and a free-market approach. Her **September 2022 mini-budget** proposed £45 billion in tax cuts, funded by borrowing, which triggered **market turmoil**, a **sterling crash**, and a **rise in government borrowing costs**.
2. **Immediate Fallout:** The mini-budget led to **pension fund instability**, **Bank of England intervention**, and **international criticism** (IMF, US President Biden). Truss was forced to **reverse most policies** and dismiss her Chancellor, Kwasi Kwarteng, after just 38 days.
3. **Political Collapse:** Truss lost support from her **Conservative Party MPs**, with over **100 letters of no confidence** submitted. Her **Home Secretary, Suella Braverman, resigned**, and allegations of **intimidation in Parliament** further eroded her authority.
4. **Conspiracy Theories:** Post-resignation, Truss claimed her downfall was orchestrated by the **"deep state"**—a shadowy establishment within the UK’s political and financial systems. Critics dismiss this as **self-justification**, arguing her policies were simply **economically unsound**.
5. **Legacy:** Truss’s brief tenure left a **lasting impact** on UK politics, reinforcing skepticism toward **radical free-market reforms** and highlighting the **fragility of political leadership** in times of economic instability.

## Findings

### 1. Liz Truss’s Economic Policies: "Trussonomics"

#### Core Principles

- **Supply-Side Economics:** Truss believed in **reducing taxes and regulation** to stimulate economic growth, a philosophy influenced by **Ronald Reagan (Reaganomics)** and **Margaret Thatcher**.
- **Free Market Fundamentalism:** She founded the **Free Enterprise Group**, advocating for **deregulation**, **lower business taxes**, and **fewer employment laws** to attract investment.
- **Tax Cuts as Growth Drivers:** Her policies assumed that **lower taxes would pay for themselves** by spurring economic activity, a theory often criticized as **"trickle-down economics."**

#### The Mini-Budget (23 September 2022)

The centerpiece of Truss’s economic agenda was the **"Growth Plan"**, commonly known as the **mini-budget**, announced by Chancellor **Kwasi Kwarteng**. Key measures included:

- **Abolition of the 45% top income tax rate** (later reversed).
- **Cancellation of the planned corporation tax rise** (from 19% to 25%).
- **Stamp duty cuts** to boost the housing market.
- **Reversal of the National Insurance increase** introduced under Boris Johnson.
- **Energy Price Guarantee:** Capping household and business energy bills to address the **cost-of-living crisis**.

#### Market Reaction

- **Sterling Collapse:** The pound fell to its **lowest level against the US dollar since 1985** (1.03 USD/GBP).
- **Bond Market Turmoil:** UK government bond yields (gilts) **spiked**, raising borrowing costs. Pension funds faced **margin calls**, risking insolvency.
- **Bank of England Intervention:** The BoE launched a **£65 billion emergency bond-buying program** to stabilize markets.
- **International Criticism:**
  - The **IMF** called the policies **"likely to increase inequality."**
  - **US President Joe Biden** stated, **"I wasn’t the only one who thought it was a mistake."**
  - **Credit rating agencies** downgraded the UK’s economic outlook.

#### Policy Reversals

Within **three weeks**, Truss was forced to:

1. **Reinstate the 45% top tax rate** (3 October 2022).
2. **Keep the corporation tax rise** (14 October 2022).
3. **Dismiss Kwasi Kwarteng** and replace him with **Jeremy Hunt**, who **reversed most remaining tax cuts**.

By the time of her resignation, **Trussonomics was effectively dead**, as described by BBC’s Faisal Islam.

### 2. Political Missteps and Loss of Confidence

#### Internal Party Revolt

- **1922 Committee Pressure:** The **Conservative Party’s 1922 Committee** (backbench MPs) began receiving **letters of no confidence** in Truss. By 20 October, **over 100 MPs** had reportedly submitted letters, far exceeding the **54 needed** to trigger a leadership vote.
- **Suella Braverman’s Resignation:** The Home Secretary resigned on **19 October**, criticizing Truss’s leadership in her resignation letter. This **accelerated the collapse** of Truss’s authority.
- **Fracking Vote Controversy:** On 19 October, Conservative MPs **rebelled** against a government motion to ban fracking. Allegations emerged of **"manhandling" and intimidation** by government whips to force MPs to vote against the ban, further damaging Truss’s reputation.

#### Public and Media Backlash

- **Polling Disaster:** Truss’s approval ratings **plummeted**. A YouGov poll on **5 October** gave her a **net favourability of -59**, worse than Boris Johnson’s lowest ratings.
- **Media Ridicule:** The **Daily Star’s lettuce livestream** (comparing her tenure’s longevity to a head of lettuce) became a **symbol of her political fragility**. The lettuce "outlasted" her premiership.
- **Satirical Backlash:** Effigies of Truss were **burned on Guy Fawkes Night**, and her resignation was met with **widespread mockery** in British media.

### 3. The "Deep State" Conspiracy Theory

#### Truss’s Claims

After her resignation, Truss began **promoting conspiracy theories** about her downfall:

- **"Deep State" Interference:** In **February 2024**, at the **Conservative Political Action Conference (CPAC)** in the US, Truss claimed her premiership was sabotaged by **"agents of the deep state"**—a supposed **shadowy establishment** within the UK’s civil service, financial institutions, and media.
- **"Secret Left" Allegations:** She argued that **left-wing forces** within the **"administrative state"** actively worked against her policies.
- **Alliances with Far-Right Figures:** Truss appeared alongside **Steve Bannon** (former Trump advisor) and aligned with **far-right groups**, reinforcing her **anti-establishment rhetoric**.

#### Counterarguments

- **Institute for Government:** Dismissed her claims as **"delusions"** and **"excuses for failure."** They argued that her policies **failed on their own merits** and that her party **lost confidence** in her leadership.
- **Mainstream Media:** Outlets like **The Conversation** and **LBC** described her conspiracy theories as **"self-justification"** and **"populist rhetoric"** to shift blame away from her **economic mismanagement**.
- **Lack of Evidence:** No credible evidence supports the existence of a **coordinated "deep state" effort** to remove Truss. Critics note that her **own MPs, the markets, and the public** turned against her **independently**.

#### Why the Conspiracy Theory Resonates

- **Populist Appeal:** Truss’s rhetoric taps into **anti-establishment sentiment**, portraying herself as a **maverick outsider** fighting against **elite control**.
- **Self-Preservation:** By blaming external forces, Truss **avoids accountability** for her policy failures.
- **Political Strategy:** Her alignment with **US conservative movements** (e.g., CPAC, Bannon) suggests an attempt to **rebrand herself** as a **global anti-establishment figure**.

### 4. Was There More to Her Removal Than the Official Reason?

#### The Official Narrative

The **primary reasons** for Truss’s resignation were:

1. **Economic Mismanagement:** The mini-budget **destabilized financial markets**, leading to **unsustainable borrowing costs** and **pension fund risks**.
2. **Loss of Party Support:** Conservative MPs **withdrew confidence** due to her **policy failures** and **poor leadership**.
3. **Public Backlash:** Polls showed **overwhelming disapproval**, and her **authority collapsed** after Braverman’s resignation.

#### Alternative Theories


| **Theory**               | **Proponents**            | **Evidence**                                             | **Credibility**                                                             |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Deep State Conspiracy    | Liz Truss, Steve Bannon   | Claims of shadowy establishment interference             | Low (No credible evidence)                                                  |
| Financial Elite Sabotage | Truss supporters          | Suggests Bank of England/IMF deliberately undermined her | Low (Markets reacted to policy risks, not conspiracy)                       |
| Internal Party Coup      | Some Conservative critics | Argues MPs exploited the crisis to remove her            | Moderate (Party infighting was real, but economic failure was the catalyst) |
| Media Witch Hunt         | Truss allies              | Claims media exaggerated failures                        | Low (Media criticism was widespread but reactive, not causative)            |


#### Assessment

- **No Credible Evidence of a Conspiracy:** While Truss and her allies **promote the "deep state" narrative**, there is **no verifiable proof** of a coordinated effort to remove her beyond the **natural consequences of her policies**.
- **Party Politics Played a Role:** The **Conservative Party’s internal dynamics** (fear of electoral annihilation, factional disputes) **accelerated her downfall**, but these were **reactions to her failures**, not the **root cause**.
- **Market Forces Were Decisive:** The **financial instability** triggered by the mini-budget was the **immediate catalyst** for her resignation. Without this, the **party revolt might not have gained momentum**.
