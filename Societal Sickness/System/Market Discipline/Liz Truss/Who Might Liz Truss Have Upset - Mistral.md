Liz Truss, politician

Here’s a short, simple summary of how Liz Truss’s policies (including those not implemented) would affect each class:

---
### Tax Cut
#### 💎 Big winners: Upper Class (Wealthy, Business Owners, High Earners)
- Huge tax cuts (45p rate scrapped, corporation tax frozen at 19%).
- Bankers’ bonus cap removed.
- Stamp duty cuts. 

#### 🏡 Mixed: Middle Class (Professionals, Homeowners, Moderate Earners)
- Some tax cuts (National Insurance, basic income tax), but stamp duty help mostly aids higher-end buyers.
- Mortgages soared due to market chaos, wiping out gains.
- Energy bill help was universal but capped.

#### 👷 Losers: Working Class (Low-Medium Earners, Renters, Public Sector Workers)
- No real benefit from tax cuts (too low to pay much tax).
- Wages stagnated, inflation hit hardest.
- public services (NHS, schools) faced cuts.
- Mortgage/rent costs rose sharply.

#### 🚨 Biggest losers: Poorest (Unemployed, Benefit Claimants, Lowest Paid)
- No tax cuts (earn too little).
- Benefits not raised in line with inflation.
- Energy bills still unaffordable after cap.
- Sanctions and austerity (planned) would tighten the squeeze.

#### 📉 Overall Effect
- Wealth flowed upward – Rich got richer, poor got poorer.
- Inequality widened – Policies were "trickle-down" in theory, but trickle-up in practice.
- Economic chaos – Markets crashed, mortgages spiked, everyone (except the richest) paid the price.

### Diplomacy
Liz Truss, as Foreign Secretary (2021–22) and later Prime Minister (2022), threatened to override parts of the Northern Ireland Protocol—a key part of the Brexit withdrawal agreement—which risked breaking international law. Here’s the short, clear breakdown:

#### 📜 What’s the Northern Ireland Protocol?
- A 2020 UK-EU deal to avoid a hard border between Northern Ireland (UK) and Ireland (EU).
- Kept NI aligned with EU goods rules—to prevent checks on the Irish land border.
- Meant checks on goods moving from Great Britain (England/Scotland/Wales) to Northern Ireland (e.g., food, medicines).

#### ⚠️ Truss’s Problem With It
- Unionists (DUP) hated it - Said it separated NI from GB, undermining UK unity.
- Businesses complained - Extra paperwork, delays, costs.
- Truss (as PM) called it "unworkable" and vowed to scrap it.

#### 💥 How She Threatened to Break International Law
1. 2022: The "Northern Ireland Protocol Bill"
   - As Foreign Secretary, Truss drafted a bill to unilaterally change the Protocol** (without EU agreement).
   - Key move: Let UK goods flow freely into NI *without EU-required checks*.
   - Legal risk: The UK had signed a treaty (the Protocol)—changing it alone = breach of international law.

2. Justification: "Necessity"
   - Truss argued the Protocol harmed NI’s economy and peace process.
   - Claimed the UK had a right to act if the deal caused "socio-economic harm" (a weak legal argument).

3. EU’s Reaction: Furious
   - EU called it illegal – Threatened trade sanctions (e.g., tariffs on UK exports).
   - US warned against it – Biden’s team said it risked UK-US relations (and the Good Friday Agreement).

4. Truss’s Defiance
   - As PM, she pushed the bill forward (though it stalled after her resignation).
   - Said: "We cannot have a situation where the EU can unilaterally decide our trade rules."

#### ⚖️ Why It Was a Big Deal
| Issue | Truss’s Stance | Who It Upset |
|-----------|-------------------|------------------|
| Breaking a treaty | "We’ll do it anyway" | EU, Ireland, international lawyers |
| Risking trade war | "UK sovereignty first" | Businesses (fear of EU tariffs) |
| Ignoring US concerns | "We’ll handle it" | Biden, US Democrats |
| Undermining peace | "NI comes first" | Nationalists (Ireland), moderates |

#### 🎯 What Actually Happened?
- The bill was introduced (June 2022) but never passed (Truss resigned in Oct 2022).
- Sunak replaced her and negotiated a new deal (Windsor Framework, Feb 2023) to void breaking the law.
- Result: Truss’s hardline approach failed—but it poisoned UK-EU relations for months.

#### 💬 Truss’s Words (Direct Quotes)
> "The current situation is unsustainable. We need to fix the Protocol." (2022)
>
> "No UK government could accept a situation where the EU can block goods moving within our own country." (Justifying the bill)

##### TL;DR:
Truss threatened to tear up a treaty to please Brexiteers, risking a trade war with the EU and angering the US. It was bold, illegal, and ultimately failed—but it showed her willingness to break global rules for political gain.
