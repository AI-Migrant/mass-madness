# Who Might Jeremy Corbyn Have Upset?

*A deep-research analysis of political divisions, controversies, and alienated groups during Corbyn's leadership*

---

## Question

Who are the key individuals, groups, institutions, and communities that Jeremy Corbyn upset during his political career, particularly as Leader of the Labour Party (2015–2020), and what were the primary sources of contention?

---

## Executive Summary

Jeremy Corbyn's leadership of the Labour Party (2015–2020) was marked by profound polarization, alienating numerous groups across the political spectrum. The most significant and documented tensions emerged with:

1. **The Jewish Community and its representative organizations** – over perceived failure to address antisemitism within Labour ranks
2. **Moderate Labour MPs and the party establishment** – over ideological direction, Brexit policy, and leadership style
3. **The British media, especially right-wing outlets** – over critical coverage and perceived bias
4. **The monarchy and royalists** – over republican sentiments and perceived disrespect
5. **The security and intelligence establishment** – over historical links to controversial groups and anti-nuclear stance
6. **Pro-Remain Labour MPs and voters** – over ambiguous Brexit positioning
7. **The business community** – over radical economic policies
8. **NATO and Western allies** – over defense and foreign policy positions

These tensions were not merely political disagreements but often involved deeply personal and institutional conflicts that reshaped British politics.

## Methodology

This report synthesizes evidence from:

- **Primary sources**: Parliamentary records, official reports (EHRC), and direct statements from involved parties
- **News archives**: BBC, The Independent, Sky News, Express, and international Jewish press (2015–2026)
- **Academic and policy analysis**: Brookings Institution, parliamentary committee evidence
- **Encyclopedic sources**: Wikipedia entries on Corbyn, Labour Party leadership, and antisemitism controversies

**Search strategy**: 

- Broad mapping of Corbyn controversies

**Limitations**: This report focuses on documented, source-backed controversies. It does not adjudicate the merits of claims but presents verified positions and reactions.

## Findings

### 1. The Jewish Community and Antisemitism Controversy

**Who was upset**: The Board of Deputies of British Jews, Jewish Labour Movement (JLM), Jewish MPs (notably Luciana Berger), Chief Rabbi Ephraim Mirvis, and the broader UK Jewish community.

**Nature of the upset**:

- **Perceived institutional failure**: Corbyn was accused of being slow to acknowledge and address antisemitism within Labour ranks. The Equalities and Human Rights Commission (EHRC) 2020 report found Labour responsible for unlawful acts of harassment and discrimination related to antisemitism.
- **Personal experiences**: Jewish Labour MPs, particularly Luciana Berger, reported receiving antisemitic abuse from Corbyn supporters. Berger stated Labour had become "sickeningly, institutionally racist" under Corbyn's leadership.
- **Symbolic controversies**: Corbyn's attendance at events with controversial figures, wreath-laying ceremonies, and associations with groups like Hamas and Hezbollah (pre-leadership) were cited as evidence of problematic judgment.
- **Response to complaints**: Corbyn's statement that antisemitism claims were "overstated for political reasons" led to his suspension from Labour in October 2020. The Board of Deputies welcomed this suspension.

**Key moments**:

- 2016: Shami Chakrabarti inquiry into antisemitism, criticized as "compromised" by Jewish groups
- 2018: Adoption of IHRA definition of antisemitism after prolonged resistance
- 2019: Chief Rabbi Mirvis's unprecedented intervention in general election, stating Corbyn was "unfit to lead" due to Labour's antisemitism crisis
- February 2019: Nine Labour MPs quit, with Berger explicitly citing "institutionally antisemitic" culture
- October 2020: EHRC report publication and Corbyn's subsequent suspension

**Quotes**:

> "I am the Member of Parliament for Liverpool Wavertree. I cannot remain in a party that I have today come to the sickening conclusion is institutionally antisemitic." – **Luciana Berger MP**, February 2019

> "The Labour Party has publicly condemned antisemitism and taken steps to address concerns, but the issue remains a subject of ongoing debate and controversy." – **Wikipedia summary of Labour antisemitism controversies**

### 2. Moderate Labour MPs and Party Establishment

**Who was upset**: Labour MPs including Luciana Berger, Chuka Umunna, Chris Leslie, Angela Smith, Gavin Shuker, Mike Gapes, Ann Coffey, Ian Austin, John Woodcock, Frank Field, and numerous others who resigned or were critical.

**Nature of the upset**:

- **Ideological divide**: Corbyn's hard-left policies (renationalization, wealth taxes, unilateral disarmament) alienated centrist and right-wing Labour MPs
- **Brexit policy**: Corbyn's ambiguous stance (personally pro-Leave but leading a Remain campaign) frustrated both sides. His "lukewarm" campaigning was criticized by Alan Johnson (Labour In for Britain chair)
- **Leadership challenges**: 2016 no-confidence motion saw 172 Labour MPs vote against Corbyn, triggering a leadership contest he won against Owen Smith
- **Mass resignations**: February 2019 saw nine MPs quit to form The Independent Group, explicitly citing Corbyn's handling of Brexit and antisemitism
- **Party discipline**: Corbyn's history of rebellious voting (428 times against the whip under New Labour) set a tone that frustrated party managers

**Key moments**:

- June 2016: 172 MPs vote no confidence in Corbyn's leadership
- September 2016: Corbyn wins re-election as leader against Owen Smith with 61.8% of membership vote
- February 2019: Formation of The Independent Group by resigning Labour MPs
- Throughout: Continuous briefing against Corbyn by party officials (documented in leaked Labour report)

**Quotes**:

> "Labour, she said, had become 'sickeningly, institutionally racist' under the tenure of its leader, Jeremy Corbyn." – **Luciana Berger**, cited in Brookings analysis

> "At times it felt as if Corbyn's office was working against the rest of the party and had conflicting objectives." – **Alan Johnson**, Labour In for Britain campaign chair

---

### 3. The British Media

**Who was upset**: Right-wing newspapers (Daily Mail, The Sun, Daily Telegraph), centrist outlets (The Times), and parts of the broadcast media (BBC, Sky News).

**Nature of the upset**:

- **Critical coverage**: Corbyn received sustained negative coverage, particularly from right-wing press
- **Perceived bias**: Corbyn and supporters accused media of coordinated attacks and misrepresentation
- **Personal attacks**: Coverage focused on Corbyn's appearance, past associations, and perceived extremism
- **Documentary controversies**: Films like "Oh Jeremy Corbyn: The Big Lie" (2023) alleged media conspiracy, while mainstream outlets rejected these claims

**Key moments**:

- 2015–2020: Sustained negative front pages in Daily Mail and Sun
- 2019: Media scrutiny intensified during general election campaign
- 2023: Glastonbury Festival cancels screening of pro-Corbyn documentary after complaint from Board of Deputies

**Quotes**:

> "The film presents events as a conspiracy against Corbyn and the anti-semitism allegations as inaccurate." – **Wikipedia on "Oh Jeremy Corbyn: The Big Lie"**

> "Marie van der Zyl of the Board of Deputies of British Jews stated that the film promoted hatred and falsely accused the Board of Deputies of being involved in a conspiracy against Jeremy Corbyn." – **Documentary controversy**

### 4. The Monarchy and Royalists

**Who was upset**: Monarchists, royal family supporters, conservative establishment, and parts of the Conservative Party.

**Nature of the upset**:

- **Republican sentiments**: Corbyn's long-standing republican views and co-sponsorship of Tony Benn's Commonwealth of Britain Bill (1996) which aimed to abolish the monarchy
- **Symbolic gestures**: Failure to bow to Queen Elizabeth II, refusal to sing "God Save the Queen" at public events
- **Critical statements**: 2001 interview unearthed where Corbyn called for elected head of state to replace monarchy; 2019 comments about scaling back Royal Family
- **Perceived disrespect**: Delayed tribute to Prince Philip in 2021, criticized as "dreadful timing"

**Key moments**:

- 1996: Co-signs Tony Benn's bill to abolish monarchy
- 2015: Stands silently during "God Save the Queen" at Battle of Britain event
- 2017: When asked about abolishing monarchy: "There is nothing in there because we are not going to do it"
- 2019: Calls for Royal Family to be "scaled back"
- April 2021: Criticized for slow response to Prince Philip's death

**Quotes**:

> "Jeremy Corbyn wants to replace the Queen with an elected head of state once the monarch dies... The left-winger has said he believes it is 'time to move on' and 'call it a day' on the Royal family." – **Daily Express**

> "Jeremy Corbyn snubbed Queen with 'deeply insulting' refusal... This resulted in criticism, and led to Mr Corbyn deleting the tweet before posting a tribute to the Duke of Edinburgh." – **Daily Express**

### 5. Security and Intelligence Establishment

**Who was upset**: MI5, Metropolitan Police Special Branch, military establishment, and security services.

**Nature of the upset**:

- **Historical surveillance**: Corbyn was monitored by Special Branch for two decades until early 2000s as "deemed to be a subversive"
- **MI5 file**: MI5 opened file on Corbyn in early 1990s to monitor his links to IRA
- **Anti-nuclear stance**: Long-standing opposition to Trident nuclear weapons and NATO membership
- **Associations**: Links to IRA/Sinn Féin during The Troubles, including meeting Gerry Adams and inviting Sinn Féin members to Westminster
- **Stop the War Coalition**: Leadership role in anti-war organization criticized for association with controversial figures

**Key moments**:

- 1980s: Multiple meetings with Gerry Adams and Sinn Féin members
- 1984: Invited Adams and convicted IRA volunteers to Westminster two weeks after IRA bombing of Conservative Party conference
- 1987: Attended commemoration for IRA members killed by SAS, calling for British troops to withdraw from Northern Ireland
- 1990s: MI5 opens file on Corbyn over IRA links
- 2001: Elected to Stop the War Coalition steering committee

**Quotes**:

> "The Metropolitan Police's Special Branch monitored Corbyn for two decades, until the early 2000s, as he was 'deemed to be a subversive'. According to the Labour Party, 'The Security Services kept files on many peace and Labour movement campaigners at the time, including anti-Apartheid activists and trade unionists'." – **Jeremy Corbyn Wikipedia**

> "In the early 1990s, MI5 opened a file on Corbyn to monitor his links to the IRA." – **Jeremy Corbyn Wikipedia**

### 6. Pro-Remain Voters and MPs

**Who was upset**: Labour MPs who supported Remain, pro-EU Labour members, and the 48% of voters who backed Remain in 2016 referendum.

**Nature of the upset**:

- **Ambiguous leadership**: Corbyn personally voted Remain but was accused of "lukewarm" campaigning
- **Holiday during campaign**: Took holiday during EU referendum campaign, criticized by Phil Wilson (Labour In for Britain chair)
- **Conflicting objectives**: Alan Johnson stated Corbyn's office appeared to be "working against the rest of the party"
- **Policy confusion**: Corbyn's historical Euroscepticism (called EU "a conspiracy of business people") conflicted with official Remain position
- **Diane Abbott revelation**: "Jeremy in his heart of hearts is a Brexiter"

**Key moments**:

- June 2016: Corbyn goes on holiday during referendum campaign
- June 2016: Labour In for Britain campaign struggles with Corbyn's ambiguous support
- 2017–2019: Corbyn's evolving Brexit position (from respecting result to supporting second referendum) alienates both sides

**Quotes**:

> "At times it felt as if Corbyn's office was working against the rest of the party and had conflicting objectives." – **Alan Johnson**

> "Diane Abbott, one of Corbyn's key allies, later said 'Jeremy in his heart of hearts is a Brexiter'. She said Corbyn was hostile to the European Union, which he considered 'a conspiracy of business people'." – **Wikipedia**

### 7. The Business Community

**Who was upset**: Confederation of British Industry (CBI), business leaders, City of London institutions, and corporate sector.

**Nature of the upset**:

- **Radical economic policies**: Proposals for wealth taxes, renationalization of utilities and railways, and increased corporation taxes
- **Anti-business rhetoric**: Long-standing criticism of capitalism and corporate power
- **Uncertainty**: Business community expressed concerns about Corbyn's economic policies creating instability
- **Investment concerns**: Threats of capital flight and reduced investment under Corbyn's proposed policies

**Key moments**:

- 2015–2020: Sustained business community opposition to Corbyn's economic platform
- 2019: CBI and business groups express concerns during general election
- Post-2019: Ironically, many of Corbyn's 2019 tax policies were later implemented by Conservative government

**Quotes**:

> "In 2023, The Daily Telegraph reported that most of the tax policies in Corbyn's 2019 general election manifesto had been implemented by the winning Conservative government, including a higher corporation tax, a windfall tax on oil companies, a reduction in annual tax allowances on dividend income, raising income tax on high earners, and introducing a digital services tax on online retailers." – **Political positions of Jeremy Corbyn**

---

### 8. NATO and Western Allies

**Who was upset**: NATO member states, US government, UK defense establishment, and Atlanticist politicians.

**Nature of the upset**:

- **NATO opposition**: Corbyn's long-standing call for UK to leave NATO
- **Unilateral disarmament**: Support for scrapping Trident nuclear weapons system
- **Anti-interventionism**: Opposition to military interventions, including Iraq War (which he later apologized for Labour supporting)
- **Russia relations**: Criticized for perceived softness on Russia and controversial statements on international issues
- **Alliance reliability**: Concerns that Corbyn as PM would undermine UK's reliability as NATO ally

**Key moments**:

- 2003: Opposition to Iraq War, later apologizes on behalf of Labour Party
- 2015–2020: Continued calls for NATO withdrawal and nuclear disarmament
- 2019: Criticism during election campaign over national security stance

**Quotes**:

> "Jeremy Corbyn and those around him are on the wrong side on so many international issues – from Russia, to Syria, to Venezuela. A Corbyn Labour government would threaten our national security and international alliances." – **Chuka Umunna**, February 2019

---

### 9. Conservative Party and Right-Wing Politicians

**Who was upset**: Conservative Party MPs, leadership, and right-wing political figures.

**Nature of the upset**:

- **Political opposition**: Standard partisan conflict amplified by Corbyn's radical platform
- **Personal attacks**: Conservative politicians frequently criticized Corbyn's character, associations, and fitness for office
- **National security concerns**: Conservative government and MPs highlighted Corbyn's past associations as security risks
- **Economic policy opposition**: Strong opposition to Corbyn's tax and spend proposals

**Key moments**:

- 2015–2020: Sustained Conservative Party attacks on Corbyn's leadership
- 2019: Conservative campaign focused heavily on Corbyn's perceived extremism and economic policies

---

### 10. Internal Labour Party Apparatus

**Who was upset**: Labour Party officials, staff, and moderate party members.

**Nature of the upset**:

- **Leaked report**: 2020 leaked internal Labour report ("The Work of the Labour Party's Governance and Legal Unit in Relation to Antisemitism, 2014–2019") alleged systematic opposition to Corbyn from party officials
- **Sabotage claims**: Report claimed party officials worked to undermine Corbyn's leadership
- **Factionalism**: Deep divisions between Corbyn's team and party establishment
- **Disciplinary processes**: Accusations that party bureaucracy was weaponized against Corbyn supporters

**Key moments**:

- April 2020: Leaked report details alleged opposition from party officials
- 2015–2020: Continuous tensions between Corbyn's office and Labour HQ

**Conflicts and caveats**:

- The leaked Labour report on internal opposition to Corbyn is itself contested, with both sides disputing its accuracy and motivations

## Open Questions

1. **Scale vs. perception of antisemitism**: To what extent was Labour's antisemitism problem a genuine crisis versus a politically weaponized issue? The EHRC report confirmed unlawful acts, but debate continues over scale and motivations.
2. **Media role**: Did media coverage of Corbyn cross from legitimate criticism into coordinated misrepresentation? This remains a contested narrative.
3. **Internal Labour sabotage**: How much of Corbyn's difficulties were self-inflicted versus the result of systematic opposition from party officials? The leaked report suggests significant internal resistance.
4. **Electoral impact**: To what extent did these controversies contribute to Labour's 2019 election defeat? Analysis varies on the relative weight of Brexit, antisemitism, and Corbyn's personal unpopularity.
5. **Long-term legacy**: Will Corbyn be remembered as a transformative figure who shifted UK politics leftward, or as a divisive leader who damaged Labour's electoral prospects?
