# Government PsyOps on the Internet

## 1. Introduction

Psychological operations (PsyOps) have evolved from leaflets, radio, and television into a dense web of online influence: social media campaigns, algorithmic targeting, deepfakes, and coordinated disinformation. In the digital era, governments and state-linked actors use the internet not just to inform or persuade, but to shape perceptions, fracture trust, and steer political behavior at scale.

This report focuses on government-led or government-directed PsyOps on the internet: their methods, goals, notable case studies, ethical implications, and impacts on democracy, civil society, and international relations.

---

## 2. Defining PsyOps and the Digital Information Environment

### 2.1 Classical PsyOps

Psychological operations are planned activities that convey selected information to influence the emotions, motives, reasoning, and ultimately the behavior of target audiences. Traditionally, they have been associated with military and intelligence organizations and used in wartime or crisis settings.

Key features:
- **Intentionality:** Deliberate design to change attitudes or behavior.
- **Targeting:** Specific audiences (foreign populations, adversary forces, domestic groups).
- **Integration:** Often coordinated with military, diplomatic, or economic actions.

### 2.2 From Information Operations to Cognitive Warfare

The internet has expanded PsyOps into what many militaries now call **information operations** or **cognitive warfare**—campaigns that target how people interpret reality and make decisions.

Core ideas:
- **Cognitive domain as battlespace:** Perception, belief formation, and trust become strategic targets.
- **Persistent operations:** Continuous, low-level campaigns rather than episodic wartime efforts.
- **Hybrid warfare:** PsyOps are integrated with cyberattacks, economic pressure, and conventional force.

---

## 3. Goals of Government PsyOps Online

Governments use internet-based PsyOps for overlapping strategic goals:

- **Controlling narratives**
- **Influencing public opinion**
- **Destabilizing adversaries**
- **Reinforcing regime security**
- **Strategic signaling**

---

## 4. Methods and Techniques of Online PsyOps

### 4.1 Disinformation Campaigns

Disinformation is false or misleading information deliberately created or spread to deceive.

Mechanisms:
- Fabricated news sites
- Coordinated inauthentic behavior
- Narrative “ground-shifting”

### 4.2 Social Media Manipulation

Governments exploit social platforms to reach and segment audiences:

- Botnets and troll farms
- Microtargeting
- Hashtag hijacking and trend seeding

### 4.3 Cyber Influence Strategies

These blend technical operations with psychological effects:

- Hack-and-leak operations
- Website defacement and spoofing
- Deepfakes and synthetic media

### 4.4 Domestic Propaganda and Censorship

Many governments combine outward-facing PsyOps with inward-facing control:

- State-controlled media ecosystems
- Content filtering and firewalls
- Legal and technical repression

## 6. Case Studies

### 6.1 Russia: Internet Research Agency & Operation Doppelgänger

**Internet Research Agency (IRA)**  
- Kremlin-linked organization running large-scale social media influence operations.  
- Fake personas posing as activists, coordinated posting, targeting both left- and right-wing communities.  
- Goals: amplify polarization, erode trust, influence elections.

**Operation Doppelgänger**  
- Fabricated legitimate-looking news websites amplified by bots.  
- Forced governments and media to respond, normalizing Russian information-space penetration.

### 6.2 China: Cognitive Warfare & Global Media Networks

- Extensive censorship and platform guidance domestically.  
- Cognitive warfare doctrine: shaping mental states by controlling informational stimuli.  
- External influence via state media, social platforms, and content partnerships.

### 6.3 Iran: Covert Media & Regional Influence

- State-linked networks posing as independent media.  
- Narratives: anti-US, anti-Israel, pro-Iran regional positioning.  
- Often synchronized with cyber operations.

### 6.4 Hybrid Warfare Examples (Russia–Ukraine, China–Taiwan)

- Russia–Ukraine: disinformation about troop movements, atrocities, legitimacy; deepfakes; spoofed sites.  
- China–Taiwan: pressure campaigns combining military signaling, economic leverage, and online narratives.

## 11. Conclusion

Government PsyOps on the internet have transformed the cognitive domain into a central arena of modern power politics. Through disinformation, social media manipulation, cyber influence operations, and domestic propaganda, states can destabilize adversaries, reinforce regime security, and shape global narratives at relatively low cost and high deniability.

The ethical and political stakes are high: these operations can erode democratic processes, fracture civil society, and complicate international relations. At the same time, awareness of these tactics has spurred new forms of resilience—technical defenses, legal frameworks, and societal efforts to rebuild trust and shared reality.
