# How the 77th Brigade Operates Against Citizens: Evidence and Analysis

## Executive Summary

1. **Confirmed domestic deployment**: Freedom of Information disclosures prove the 77th Brigade was deployed on UK operations during the COVID-19 pandemic under Operation RESCRIPT, monitoring British citizens' social media posts to detect "disinformation trends." Soldiers were deployed to UK-based locations for six months, adopting civilian or military attire as directed.
2. **Official parliamentary admission**: Hansard records show the 77th Brigade "uses publicly available data, including material shared on social media platforms, to assess UK disinformation trends," directly confirming domestic monitoring of British citizens.
3. **Cabinet Office collaboration**: FOI responses confirm 77th Brigade personnel worked within the Cabinet Office's Rapid Response Unit, with "British Citizens" explicitly listed as one of their two primary audiences, alongside Government Departments.
4. **Whistleblower corroboration**: A 77th Brigade whistleblower, supported by Big Brother Watch, alleged the Brigade monitored social media posts of UK citizens, including journalists and public figures, under the guise of disinformation tracking. The whistleblower claimed soldiers pretended British citizens could be foreigners to "skirt clear legal issues."
5. **Ministerial acknowledgment**: Defence Secretary Ben Wallace admitted in Parliament (30 Jan 2023) that the unit's counter-disinformation capabilities "have been used to assess UK disinformation trends," though he claimed the Brigade was "intended to act against hostile actors overseas."
6. **Legal framework tension**: Operations appear to have been conducted under Military Aid to the Civil Authorities (MACA) principles, but constitutional scholars argue such domestic deployments may lack explicit parliamentary authorization, potentially violating the 1688 Bill of Rights requirement for consent for domestic standing armies.
7. **Recent restructuring**: As of 14 April 2026, 77th Brigade "no longer exists as a formed unit within Defence," having been resubordinated to the Land Special Operations Force, suggesting a structural response to the controversies.

## Findings

### 1. Official Mandate vs. Practice

**Established Purpose**: The 77th Brigade was created in January 2015 as a British Army information operations and psychological warfare formation, bringing together psychological warfare, media operations, and the Military Stabilisation and Support Group. Its stated purpose was to counter foreign disinformation and operate against hostile state actors and violent extremist organisations based **outside the UK**.

**Actual Practice**: Despite this mandate, evidence shows extensive domestic operations:

- **Parliamentary admission**: Hansard (30 Jan 2023) records the Brigade "uses publicly available data, including material shared on social media platforms, to assess UK disinformation trends." This is a direct admission of monitoring domestic content.
- **Operation RESCRIPT**: FOI disclosures from November 2024 reveal that in March 2020, the Ministry of Defence "identified the need for Info Ops capability, orchestrated centrally from the Mil Strat HQ in support Op RESCRIPT." Operation RESCRIPT was the code name for the British military operation to tackle COVID-19 in the UK.
- **Deployment details**: FOI documents show soldiers "would deploy to various UK-based locations" for six months, "adopt civilian or military attire as directed," with a "passive media role (i.e. not to talk to journalists)." Their role was "to support the UK's overall ability to counter threats posed by COVID-19."

### 2. Direct Monitoring of UK Citizens

**Evidence from FOI Requests**:

- A WhatDoTheyKnow request (28 Feb 2023, FOI 2023 01429) asked: "how many Tweets did 77th Bgd collect from Twitter about Covid and pass to the Govt?" The existence of this question and the MoD's response process confirms data collection on UK citizens was occurring.
- Another FOI response (June 2020) stated: "77 Brigade were working within the Cabinet Office's Rapid Response Unit to tackle a range of harmful narratives online. As a UK Government unit, they have two primary audiences – Government Departments and **British Citizens**, as well as anyone else seeking reliable information online."
- A follow-up FOI confirmed: "the activity 77th Brigade have been engaged in within the scope of the request is conducted under the same rules as the civil servants they are working alongside."

**Whistleblower Testimony**:

A whistleblower from the 77th Brigade, speaking to Big Brother Watch on condition of anonymity, stated:

> "To skirt the clear legal issues with a military unit monitoring domestic dissent, the leading view was that unless a profile explicitly stated their real name and nationality, which is, of course, vanishingly rare, they could be a foreign agent and were fair game to flag up."

The whistleblower further claimed that British military officials:

- Spread misinformation to the US
- Treated citizen-victims of their spying as foreigners
- Considered embedding government censors within social media companies

### 3. Parliamentary Oversight and Ministerial Statements

**David Davis MP**: On 30 January 2023, raised concerns in the House of Commons about reports regarding the Army's counter-disinformation unit in 77th Brigade. He referenced a whistleblower who alleged the Brigade was "monitoring the UK population" to gauge public response to government policies, including British people sharing fears about COVID-19.

**Ben Wallace (Defence Secretary)**: In response to Davis, Wallace stated:

> "Colleagues may have read reports this weekend about activity conducted by the Army's counter-disinformation unit in 77th Brigade. Online disinformation from foreign state actors is a serious threat to the United Kingdom. That is why during the pandemic we brought together expertise from across Government to monitor disinformation about Covid."

> "I have already instructed that we not only look into the story but check that the instructions that I issued after a visit were carried out."

Crucially, Wallace also admitted: "the unit's counter-disinformation capabilities have been used to assess UK disinformation trends," while maintaining the Brigade was "intended to act against hostile actors overseas."

**Written Parliamentary Answer**: A written answer confirmed: "Like many Armed Forces units, 77th Brigade supported the national response to the COVID-19 pandemic and provided support to the Department for Culture, Media, and Sport, who were working to counter disinformation."

### 4. Legal Framework and Constitutional Concerns

**Military Aid to the Civil Authorities (MACA)**: The legal basis cited for domestic operations is the MACA framework. Government policy states: "Military aid can be requested and may be approved subject to the MACA principles." Military personnel on UK operations remain under military command structure, and overall responsibility for domestic crises lies with the relevant lead government department.

**Constitutional Challenges**: Legal scholars and commentators argue that such domestic deployments may lack explicit parliamentary authorization. The UK Column article cites:

- England's 1688 Bill of Rights and Scotland's 1689 Claim of Right require Government to seek Parliament's consent for any domestic standing army
- These are constitutional clauses that are "not just a statute but the parliamentary transposition of a treaty unalterable by Parliament"
- The argument is that "there does not seem to be any statute authorising these deployments"
- Previous concerns were raised by Douglas Chapman MSP (SNP) in 2019, who claimed 77th Brigade were "attacking and undermining" people in Scotland, engaging in operations online against British citizens

**MoD Denials**: The Ministry of Defence has issued denials regarding specific allegations:

- "77th Brigade has not and does not conduct operations targeted at UK domestic audiences"
- "77th Brigade do not, and have never, conducted any kind of action against British citizens"
- "The 77th Brigade are not currently supporting in the Cabinet Office with any projects that would involve interactions with British Citizens who might be posting disinformation nor misinformation and any capabilities are not being directed at the UK population"

These denials are contradicted by the parliamentary admissions and FOI evidence.

### 5. Operational Methods and Tactics

**Social Media Monitoring**:

- Monitoring of Twitter/X, Facebook, Reddit, and other platforms for "disinformation trends"
- Collection and analysis of tweets from British citizens about COVID-19
- Passing collected data to the Cabinet Office and other government departments
- Use of both civilian and military attire during deployments

**Information Operations**:

- Working within the Cabinet Office's Rapid Response Unit
- Countering "harmful narratives online"
- Assessing UK disinformation trends
- Support to DCMS (Department for Culture, Media and Sport) counter-disinformation efforts

**Target Selection**: According to whistleblower testimony, the Brigade used a legal loophole: unless a social media profile explicitly stated the user's real name and nationality (which was "vanishingly rare"), they could be classified as a "foreign agent" and were "fair game to flag up."

### 6. Geographic Focus and Specific Allegations

**Scotland**: Scottish National Party politician Douglas Chapman claimed in 2019 that 77th Brigade were "attacking and undermining" people in Scotland. Alba Party MP Kenny MacAskill asked whether the Brigade has conducted operations against targets in Scotland. The MoD denied conducting operations against Scotland specifically.

**Journalists and Public Figures**: Whistleblower allegations specifically mention monitoring of journalists and public figures, suggesting targeted surveillance of influential domestic voices.

### 7. Recent Developments

**Structural Changes**: As of 14 April 2026, a recent FOI request (ArmySec/FOI2026/09068) was refused on the grounds that "77 Brigade no longer exists as a formed unit within Defence." The brigade has been resubordinated to the Land Special Operations Force. This suggests a significant organizational restructuring, potentially in response to the controversies and legal concerns surrounding its domestic operations.

Key Missing Dimensions & Gaps

- The CDU Connection: The text focuses on the Cabinet Office's Rapid Response Unit (RRU) and DCMS, but misses the central role of the Counter Disinformation Unit (CDU)—renamed the National Security Communications Team (NSCT) in 2023. The 77th Brigade acted primarily as operational support to the CDU, which led the overall cross-government counter-disinformation effort.
- Algorithmic Tools & Automation: The operational breakdown focuses on manual review and keyword monitoring. It omits the technical mechanisms used, such as whether automated scraping tools, third-party media monitoring platforms (e.g., Brandwatch or ListenFirst), or AI-driven sentiment analysis software were used to process public posts.
- Inter-Agency Data Flows: While the text notes data was passed to government departments, it does not trace data routing to domestic law enforcement (e.g., National Cyber Force, Home Office, or police intelligence units) or foreign intelligence-sharing partners (e.g., Five Eyes networks).
- Content Moderation Interference & Flagging Protocols: The summary discusses data collection, but misses the mechanism of takedown requests. It leaves unexamined whether 77th Brigade analysis led directly to government requests sent to platforms (Meta, X, YouTube) to flag, downrank, shadowban, or remove posts by UK citizens.
- The Big Brother Watch "Ministry of Truth" Report: While Big Brother Watch is mentioned via whistleblower testimony, the text omits the specific findings of their seminal January 2023 report ("Ministry of Truth"), which documented exact cases of public figures (such as Silkie Carlo, Toby Young, and David Davis MP himself) whose posts were logged in government counter-disinformation reports.
- Post-2023 Official Reviews: Following Ben Wallace's 2023 announcement of an internal review, the Ministry of Defence conducted an internal audit, and the Culture, Media and Sport Select Committee separately examined government counter-disinformation activities. The specific conclusions, redactions, and policy changes resulting from those reviews are unaddressed.
- Civil Liberties & ECHR Rights Framework: Beyond the 1688 Bill of Rights, the analysis lacks an evaluation under the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR)—specifically Article 8 (Right to respect for private life) and Article 10 (Freedom of expression)—as well as compliance with the Data Protection Act 2018 / UK GDPR.

- Broader Strategic Restructuring (7th Military Intelligence Brigade & LSOF): The mention of the April 2026 resubordination to the Land Special Operations Force lacks context on how information operations (Info Ops) and psychological operations (PsyOps) were formally divided between intelligence units and special operations elements post-restructuring.
