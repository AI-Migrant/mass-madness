# UK Smear Campaigns: How Individuals, Companies and Organisations Assist Authorities

*Research Report | August 28, 2026*

---

## Question

How do individuals, companies, and other organisations—including charities—in the United Kingdom assist or participate in state-authorised smear campaigns against activists, political groups, and civil society organisations?

---

## Executive Summary

1. **State-Sponsored Infiltration**: The UK police deployed at least 140 undercover officers to spy on over 1,000 political groups since 1968, using deception (stolen identities of dead children, long-term intimate relationships) to undermine activist movements. These operations were sanctioned at the highest levels of government and funded by the Home Office.

2. **Public-Private Intelligence Partnerships**: Private intelligence firms like Welund (founded by ex-MI6) are hired by public authorities (Greater London Authority) and corporations (BP, Shell, oil/gas giants) to monitor activists, creating a collaborative surveillance network between state and private actors.

3. **Corporate Espionage Against Activists**: Major UK and multinational companies (Nestlé, Shell, McDonald's, RBS, British Airways, Porsche) hired private spies to infiltrate and undermine activist groups, often using ex-police officers with covert operations training.

4. **Regulatory Weaponisation**: The Charity Commission, under government pressure, attempted to defund advocacy groups like CAGE by pressuring their funders (Joseph Rowntree Charitable Trust, Roddick Foundation), with evidence of direct coordination between Cabinet ministers and the regulator.

5. **Counter-Extremism Infrastructure**: The Prevent programme and CONTEST strategy create frameworks where charities, especially Muslim-led organisations, face heightened surveillance and reputational attacks, with former Charity Commission chair William Shawcross playing a central role in targeting such groups.

6. **PR Industry Disinformation**: While primarily exposed in South Africa, UK-based PR firms like Bell Pottinger demonstrate how private communications companies can execute sophisticated smear campaigns, though direct UK government involvement remains less documented than corporate cases.

7. **Legal and Institutional Complicity**: Courts, media outlets, and regulatory bodies have at times facilitated smear campaigns through selective information sharing, coordinated messaging, and lack of accountability mechanisms.

## Findings

### 1. State-Sponsored Smear Campaigns: The Spycops Scandal

#### Scale and Scope
The British police deployed **140 undercover officers** to infiltrate and monitor **over 1,000 political groups** between 1968 and at least 2008, targeting environmental campaigns, anti-racist movements, socialist organisations, and justice campaigns. The Special Demonstration Squad (SDS) and National Public Order Intelligence Unit (NPOIU) were the primary units involved.

#### Tactics and Methods
- **Identity Theft**: Officers stole the identities of **dead children** to create plausible cover stories
- **Intimate Deception**: Multiple officers entered into **long-term sexual relationships** with activists, in some cases fathering children
- **Evidence Concealment**: At least **50 protesters were wrongly convicted** because police spies withheld exculpatory evidence
- **Justice Campaign Sabotage**: The Stephen Lawrence family's campaign for justice was **undermined by undercover officers** collecting intelligence on them

#### Authorisation and Oversight
- Operations were **funded by the Home Office** and **authorised at the highest levels of government**
- Both Labour (Harold Wilson) and Conservative governments sanctioned the activities
- Information was **shared with MI5 and other security services**
- The **Undercover Policing Inquiry (UCPI)**, launched in 2014, continues to reveal the extent of the scandal

#### Key Groups Targeted
- Environmental movements (e.g., climate campaigns)
- Anti-racist organisations (e.g., Stephen Lawrence campaign)
- Socialist Workers Party (35 different officers over 40 years)
- Troops Out Movement
- Animal rights groups
- Anti-fascist organisations

### 2. Public-Private Intelligence Collaboration

#### Private Intelligence Firms Monitoring Activists
UK institutions increasingly contract private intelligence firms to monitor political activists, creating a **public-private surveillance complex**:

- **Welund Intelligence**: Founded by a former MI6 agent, provides intelligence to:
  - **Public authorities**: Greater London Authority (£12,000 contract in 2020)
  - **Corporations**: BP and "many other oil and gas giants"
  - **Services**: Daily dashboards listing planned demonstrations and "potentially disruptive" events
  - **Claim**: Provides warnings of gatherings that authorities "did not have information on from other sources – including [the Metropolitan Police]"

- **Other Firms**: The UK private intelligence industry (valued at £15bn+ globally) employs many ex-spies, servicemen, and police officers who use state-trained covert skills for corporate clients

#### Institutional Involvement
- **Greater London Authority (GLA)**: Hired Welund to monitor environmental activists, with security officials "spying" on activists and blocking them from public debates
- **City Hall**: Used Welund intelligence to track protest groups
- **Collaboration**: Private firms explicitly state they provide information that police don't have, creating a parallel surveillance system

> **Source**: [openDemocracy: The private spies monitoring UK climate campaigners](https://www.opendemocracy.net/en/private-intelligence-spying-on-climate-campaigners-mainstream-uk/) | [Privacy International: UK institutions track protests alongside police](https://privacyinternational.org/examples/5370/uk-institutions-track-protests-alongside-police)

### 3. Corporate Espionage Against Activists

#### Major Companies and Their Intelligence Operations

**Documented Cases**:
- **Nestlé, Shell, McDonald's**: Used covert methods to gather intelligence on activist groups, counter criticism, and evade accountability (Dr. Eveline Lubbers, University of Bath)
- **RBS, British Airways, Caterpillar, Porsche**: Hired private spies to infiltrate activist groups, with undercover agents gaining trust and compiling reports
- **Gold Mining Company (Northern Ireland)**: Spent years monitoring a 72-year-old activist, amassing a 92-page dossier

**Industry Patterns**:
- **Staffing**: Firms often staffed by ex-police officers, creating direct personnel links between state and corporate surveillance
- **Targets**: Phone mast protesters, environmental activists, families of killed activists (e.g., Rachel Corrie's family)
- **Methods**: Infiltration, trust-building, comprehensive reporting on activities
- **Regulation**: Corporate intelligence is "largely unregulated" in the UK

**Academic Analysis**:
Dr. Lubbers' research ("Secret Manoeuvres in the Dark") documents how corporate intelligence gathering has shifted from reactive to proactive, with cooperation between government and corporate intelligence being a "seriously neglected field of research."

### 4. Charity Sector: Regulatory Smear Campaigns

#### The CAGE Case Study

**Background**: CAGE is an advocacy group campaigning against torture, imprisonment without trial, and anti-terror laws.

**Smear Campaign Triggers**:
- In February 2015, CAGE's research director Asim Qureshi stated that Mohamed Emwazi ("Jihadi John") had been harassed by British intelligence agencies and that this contact may have contributed to his radicalisation
- Prime Minister David Cameron, London Mayor Boris Johnson, and other senior politicians publicly condemned CAGE

**Government-Coordinated Response**:
- **Charity Commission Pressure**: The regulator **pressured the Joseph Rowntree Charitable Trust and Roddick Foundation** to cease funding CAGE
- **Direct Ministerial Involvement**: Evidence revealed that **Theresa Villiers (Cabinet Minister) and US intelligence** applied pressure on the Charity Commission
- **Media Coordination**: The Home Secretary (Theresa May) supported Daily Mail "investigations" with "direct line" to journalists, showing stories ran with Home Office approval
- **Cross-Party Involvement**: Education Secretary Nicky Morgan, Foreign Secretary Philip Hammond, and Conservative MP Jo Johnson all participated in smearing CAGE

**Regulatory Tactics**:
- **Threats**: Charity Commission threatened "exhaustive investigation" into Joseph Rowntree Charitable Trust if it refused to stop funding CAGE
- **Public Statements**: Commission claimed funding CAGE risked "damaging public trust and confidence in charity"
- **Legal Challenge**: CAGE won a **landmark High Court victory** in October 2015, with Lord Chief Justice Thomas ruling the Charity Commission had **no right to compel charities not to fund CAGE**
- **Retreat**: Following the court ruling, the Charity Commission changed its position and agreed not to interfere in charities' discretion to fund CAGE

**Funding Impact**:
- Between 2007–2014, Joseph Rowntree Charitable Trust gave CAGE £271,250
- Roddick Foundation gave £120,000 in the same period
- Both stopped funding in 2015 due to regulatory pressure, though CAGE continued through private donations

### 5. Counter-Extremism Framework: Prevent and CONTEST

#### Structural Smear Mechanisms

**Prevent Programme**:
- Part of the UK's **CONTEST counter-terrorism strategy**
- Aims to "stop people becoming terrorists or supporting terrorism"
- Includes "challenging and preventing extremist ideology"
- Became a **legal duty** for charities, education, and health sectors in 2015

**Targeting Patterns**:
- **Muslim Charities**: "Muslim charities in particular have come under scrutiny in an environment of Islamophobia and media conflation of anything 'Muslim' with 'terrorism'" (LSE analysis)
- **William Shawcross Role**: As Charity Commission chair (2012–2018), Shawcross oversaw the majority of investigations and surveillance of Muslim charities
- **Political Appointment**: Shawcross's 2021 appointment to review Prevent drew criticism from Amnesty International and other human rights groups, who saw it as evidence the government sought a non-objective review

**Government Policy**:
- The **Counter-Extremism Strategy** (2015) focuses on four areas: countering extremist ideology, building partnerships, disrupting extremists, building cohesive communities
- **2024 Definition**: Government updated its definition of extremism, with charities required to align with it

**Criticism**:
- Evidence shows "undue targeting, surveillance, and reporting of Muslim individuals as potential 'extremists'"
- The securitisation of charities is "counter-productive" to the stated goal of supporting mainstream voices in civil society

> **Source**: [LSE: Conflating charity with extremism](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/politicsandpolicy/conflating-charity-with-extremist-purposes/) | [GOV.UK: Protecting charities from extremism](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/protecting-charities-from-abuse-for-extremist-purposes)

### 6. PR Industry: Disinformation Capabilities

#### Bell Pottinger Case (UK Firm, International Smear Campaign)

While the most documented Bell Pottinger smear campaign occurred in South Africa, it demonstrates UK PR industry capabilities:

**South Africa Campaign**:
- **Client**: Gupta family (controversial business family close to President Jacob Zuma)
- **Strategy**: Created and amplified "white monopoly capital" narrative to deflect corruption allegations
- **Tactics**: Used fake bloggers, Twitter accounts, and Wikipedia edits to sow racial division
- **Scale**: Hundreds of fake social media accounts posted racial messages against Gupta opponents
- **Outcome**: Bell Pottinger was **expelled from the UK PRCA** (Public Relations and Communications Association) in September 2017
- **Collapse**: The firm went into administration, shedding all staff by November 2017

**UK Connections**:
- Bell Pottinger was a prestigious **UK-based PR firm**
- The scandal showed how UK PR firms can execute sophisticated disinformation campaigns
- Former employees faced potential director disqualification under the Company Directors Disqualification Act 1986

**Industry Implications**:
- Demonstrates that UK-based communications firms have the capability to run smear campaigns
- Shows regulatory bodies (PRCA) can take action, but typically only after public exposure
- Highlights the role of former intelligence/police personnel in the PR industry

### 7. Media Collaboration in Smear Campaigns

#### Coordinated Messaging
- **Daily Mail**: Ran "investigations" into CAGE with direct coordination from the Home Secretary's office
- **Mainstream Media**: Often amplified government narratives about activist groups without critical scrutiny
- **Social Media**: Private intelligence firms and PR companies use fake accounts to shape online narratives

#### Information Flow
- Government sources provide selective information to sympathetic journalists
- Media outlets publish stories that support official narratives
- Activist groups face reputational damage without adequate right of reply

## Mechanisms of Collaboration

### Direct State-Private Partnerships
1. **Contracting**: Public authorities hire private intelligence firms (e.g., GLA hiring Welund)
2. **Information Sharing**: Police and private firms exchange intelligence on activists
3. **Joint Operations**: Undercover officers work alongside corporate security

### Indirect Assistance
1. **Regulatory Pressure**: Government influences regulators (Charity Commission) to target specific groups
2. **Media Coordination**: Official sources provide information to sympathetic journalists
3. **Legal Framework**: Counter-terrorism laws (CONTEST, Prevent) create structures that facilitate targeting

### Personnel Overlap
1. **Revolving Door**: Ex-police, ex-intelligence officers work for private firms
2. **Training Transfer**: State-trained covert skills applied to corporate surveillance
3. **Network Effects**: Personnel connections facilitate information sharing

## Recommendations

### For Civil Society
1. **Enhanced Security**: Activist groups should implement counter-surveillance training and digital security measures
2. **Legal Support**: Establish legal defence funds for targeted organisations
3. **Whistleblower Protection**: Create secure channels for police and intelligence whistleblowers
4. **Media Literacy**: Educate members on media manipulation tactics

### For Regulators and Government
1. **Transparency**: Full disclosure of Undercover Policing Inquiry findings
2. **Regulation**: Establish oversight for private intelligence industry
3. **Accountability**: Prosecute officers and officials involved in illegal smear operations
4. **Reform**: Review and reform Prevent programme to address discriminatory targeting

### For Journalists and Researchers
1. **Investigative Focus**: Continue exposing public-private surveillance partnerships
2. **FOI Requests**: Use Freedom of Information laws to obtain government contracts with private firms
3. **Data Analysis**: Map connections between ex-state personnel and private sector surveillance
4. **International Comparison**: Study UK practices in context of other democracies

### For Charities
1. **Legal Resistance**: Challenge regulatory overreach through judicial review
2. **Collective Defence**: Establish support networks for targeted charities
3. **Transparency**: Document and publicise instances of government pressure
4. **Public Education**: Inform donors and beneficiaries about smear campaign tactics

## Conclusion

The evidence demonstrates that **individuals, companies, and organisations in the UK systematically assist authorities in smear campaigns** through multiple, interconnected mechanisms. The most extensive is the **spycops scandal**, representing decades of state-sponsored infiltration. However, the **public-private intelligence collaboration** and **corporate espionage** against activists reveal a broader ecosystem of surveillance and disinformation. The **weaponisation of charity regulation** and **counter-extremism frameworks** show how institutional structures can be co-opted for political targeting. While direct UK government-PR firm smear campaigns are less documented than corporate cases, the **Bell Pottinger scandal demonstrates the capability exists** within the UK communications industry.

The **normalisation of these practices**—through legal frameworks, regulatory pressure, and media coordination—poses a significant threat to democratic participation and civil liberties in the UK. Addressing this requires structural reforms, enhanced transparency, and robust accountability mechanisms.
