*Focused Research Report | August 28, 2026*

## Question

What specific **deliberate character assassination tactics** are used in the UK where authorities or their collaborators **stir up troubles and then accuse their targets as the problem**, including creating incidents (such as problems at checkout) to make it appear that targets shoplifted or committed other offenses?

---

## Executive Summary

1. **Agent Provocateur Operations**: Undercover police officers like **Mark Kennedy** actively **incited, encouraged, and organized illegal actions** (Ratcliffe-on-Soar power station protest), then used the resulting activity to arrest and prosecute activists. When exposed, this caused the **trial to collapse** and revealed police acting as "agents provocateurs."

2. **Deliberate Framing of Witnesses**: **Duwayne Brooks**, Stephen Lawrence's friend and key witness, was subjected to a **20-year smear campaign** where police **bugged his meetings with lawyers**, **instructed undercover officers to find discrediting information**, and **fabricated allegations** to undermine his testimony.

3. **Created Incidents for Blame**: Police **arrested Duwayne Brooks** days after Stephen Lawrence's murder on **false allegations** during an anti-racist march, then used this to **discredit him** as a witness, demonstrating the tactic of manufacturing incidents to blame targets.

4. **Evidence Planting and Withholding**: In the Ratcliffe case, police **withheld exculpatory evidence** (secret tapes) that could have proven activists' innocence, while simultaneously **concealing their undercover officer's provocateur role**—a form of creating a false narrative.

5. **Systematic Reputation Destruction**: The **Special Demonstration Squad** was explicitly tasked with **finding incriminating details** about the Stephen Lawrence justice campaign, including his **parents and friends**, to undermine their credibility.

## Findings: Specific Character Assassination Tactics

### 1. Agent Provocateur: Creating Crimes to Blame Targets

#### The Ratcliffe-on-Soar Power Station Case (2009)

**The Setup**:
- Undercover officer **Mark Kennedy** (posing as "Mark Stone") infiltrated environmental activist groups
- Kennedy **actively encouraged and helped organize** a protest to shut down Ratcliffe-on-Soar coal-fired power station
- He was described as **"not just an observer, but an agent provocateur"**—"We're not talking about someone sitting at the back of the meeting taking notes – he was in the thick of it"

**The Execution**:
- Kennedy **paid to hire a van** used to transport equipment for the protest
- He **participated in planning meetings** for months, acting as an "enthusiastic supporter"
- Police **arrested 114 people** at Iona School on April 14, 2009
- **26 activists were charged** with conspiracy to commit aggravated trespass

**The Exposure**:
- **20 activists** were initially convicted (December 2010)
- The case against **6 activists** who pleaded not guilty **collapsed** when Kennedy's role was revealed
- Kennedy **contacted the defence team** in January 2011, offering to help them
- The **Crown Prosecution Service (CPS) had withheld evidence**: Secret tapes recorded by Kennedy that could have exonerated the "deniers" (activists who claimed they hadn't agreed to join)
- CPS also **withheld that Kennedy was testifying under a false name** (Mark Stone) using a **false passport supplied by police**

**The Aftermath**:
- All **20 convictions were quashed** on appeal
- Judges ruled Kennedy had **"gone much further than the authorisation he was given"**
- CPS lawyer **Ian Cunningham faced dismissal** for failing to investigate Kennedy's involvement
- Home Secretary **Theresa May** faced parliamentary questions about Kennedy acting as an "agent provocateur"

**Tactic Analysis**:
This is a **classic agent provocateur operation** where authorities:
1. **Infiltrate** the target group
2. **Encourage and facilitate** illegal activity that wouldn't have occurred without their involvement
3. **Participate actively** in planning and execution
4. **Arrest and prosecute** the very people they incited
5. **Conceal their role** through evidence withholding and false identities

### 2. Deliberate Framing: Manufacturing Incidents to Discredit

#### The Duwayne Brooks Smear Campaign (1993–Present)

**Background**:
- Duwayne Brooks was **with Stephen Lawrence** when he was murdered in a racist attack on April 22, 1993
- He was the **key eyewitness** to the murder
- He suffered severe **post-traumatic stress disorder** from the attack

**The Framing Tactics**:

**1. Immediate Arrest on False Allegations (1993)**
- **Days after Lawrence's murder**, Brooks was **arrested** during an anti-racist march outside the British National Party headquarters in Welling
- He was **charged** with an offense
- The case was **dismissed by the judge**—the allegations were false

**2. Persistent Smear Campaign (1993–2013+)**
- **Peter Francis**, former undercover officer with the **Special Demonstration Squad (SDS)**, revealed he was **instructed to find information that could discredit Mr. Brooks**
- The SDS was **tasked with discovering incriminating details** about members of the **Justice for Stephen Lawrence campaign**, including **Brooks, his parents, and friends**
- This was a **deliberate attempt to undermine the campaign for justice**

**3. Bugging of Lawyer Meetings (1999–2000)**
- **Scotland Yard secretly recorded** meetings between Duwayne Brooks, his lawyers, and police officers
- The meetings took place at **solicitors' offices** (Deighton Guedalla in Islington)
- Brooks and his lawyers were **unaware** they were being recorded
- **Metropolitan Police Commissioner Sir Bernard Hogan-Howe** called the allegations "disgraceful" and stated: **"If the family were targeted just to discredit them – if they were targeted to target the campaign, that can never be acceptable"**

**4. Testimonial Injustice (Epistemic Injustice)**
- Police officers who arrived at the murder scene **regarded Brooks with suspicion** rather than as a witness
- Officers **failed to concentrate on Brooks** and **didn't follow up energetically** on his information
- **Nobody suggested he should accompany searches** of the area, despite him knowing where the assailants had last been seen
- This is documented as a case of **testimonial injustice**—where prejudice causes hearers to give deflated credibility to a speaker's word

**Legal and Financial Impact**:
- Brooks **sued the Metropolitan Police** in 2006
- He **won £100,000 in compensation** for his treatment
- The **Macpherson Inquiry** (1999) concluded the Metropolitan Police was "institutionally racist"
- Brooks later became a **councillor** and was appointed **OBE** in 2015 for public service

**Tactic Analysis**:
This demonstrates **multiple framing techniques**:
1. **Create false incidents** (arrest on trumped-up charges)
2. **Manufacture discrediting information** (undercover officers instructed to find dirt)
3. **Surveillance of legal communications** (bugging lawyer meetings)
4. **Undermine credibility** (treat witness as suspect, ignore testimony)
5. **Persistent campaign** (20+ years of attempts to discredit)

### 3. Evidence Manipulation: Planting and Withholding

#### Ratcliffe-on-Soar Evidence Withholding

**The Concealment**:
- **Secret tapes** recorded by Mark Kennedy were **withheld from the defence**
- These tapes **could have exonerated** the six activists who claimed they hadn't agreed to join the protest
- The **CPS concealed Kennedy's false identity** (Mark Stone) and **false passport**
- Evidence suggests **CPS itself withheld the tapes**, not just the police

**The Pattern**:
- This is part of a broader pattern where **evidence that would help targets is suppressed**
- Meanwhile, **evidence that harms targets is manufactured or emphasized**
- Creates a **false narrative** where targets appear guilty by omission of exculpatory facts

#### Historical Context: Police Perjury

**The Practice**:
- **Police perjury** ("testilying") involves officers **knowingly giving false testimony**
- Used to "make the case" against defendants when irregularities threaten acquittal
- Can include **substantive misstatements of fact to convict innocent citizens**
- Includes **procedural misstatements to justify searches and seizures**
- Represents **framing through false testimony**

### 4. COINTELPRO-Style Operations in the UK

While the US **COINTELPRO** (Counterintelligence Program) is the most infamous example of state-sponsored character assassination, UK operations show **parallel tactics**:

**Common COINTELPRO Methods**:
- **Infiltration** of target groups
- **Agent provocateur** operations
- **False information** and disinformation
- **Media manipulation** to create negative narratives
- **Legal harassment** through false arrests and prosecutions
- **Reputation destruction** through manufactured scandals

**UK Adaptations**:
- **Special Demonstration Squad**: Dedicated unit for infiltrating political groups
- **National Public Order Intelligence Unit**: Monitored and disrupted protest movements
- **Undercover officers** with false identities and backstories
- **Long-term relationship deception** to gain trust and information
- **Evidence withholding** to secure convictions

**Key Difference**: Unlike COINTELPRO (which was exposed in 1971), UK operations continued **for decades** with **less oversight** and **greater secrecy**.

## Tactics Classification

### Category 1: **Active Framing (Creating the Crime)**
- **Agent Provocateur**: Undercover officers incite crimes that wouldn't otherwise occur
- **Encouragement**: Police actively push targets toward illegal actions
- **Facilitation**: Providing resources (vans, equipment, money) for illegal activities
- **Participation**: Officers join in planning and executing the very crimes they later investigate

**UK Example**: Mark Kennedy at Ratcliffe-on-Soar

### Category 2: **Passive Framing (Manufacturing Evidence)**
- **False Arrests**: Arresting targets on trumped-up charges
- **Planted Evidence**: Placing incriminating items on targets or at scenes
- **Fabricated Testimony**: Police perjury to create false narratives
- **Withheld Evidence**: Suppressing exculpatory information

**UK Example**: Duwayne Brooks' false arrest, Kennedy's withheld tapes

### Category 3: **Reputational Framing (Destroying Credibility)**
- **Smear Campaigns**: Spreading false information to damage reputation
- **Character Assassination**: Portraying targets as criminals, extremists, or untrustworthy
- **Media Manipulation**: Feeding false stories to sympathetic journalists
- **Regulatory Pressure**: Using government agencies to defund or discredit targets

**UK Example**: SDS targeting of Stephen Lawrence campaign, Charity Commission pressure on CAGE

### Category 4: **Surveillance Framing (Creating Suspicion)**
- **Bugging Legal Meetings**: Recording lawyer-client conversations
- **Monitoring Communications**: Tracking phones, emails, social media
- **Information Gathering**: Collecting personal details to find or create dirt
- **Dossier Building**: Compiling files to use against targets when needed

**UK Example**: Bugging of Duwayne Brooks' lawyer meetings
