Domestic monitoring and intelligence gathering by the UK government are regulated by legal frameworks and carried out by specific law enforcement and intelligence agencies. Oversight mechanisms exist to balance national security and public safety with individual privacy rights.

**Key Governance Frameworks**

* **Investigatory Powers Act 2016 (IPA):** Often called the "Snoopers' Charter," this act consolidated and expanded powers for surveillance. It sets rules for targeted interception, access to communications data, and bulk data collection by intelligence agencies.
* **Regulation of Investigatory Powers Act 2000 (RIPA):** Governs covert surveillance by public bodies (including police and local councils), human intelligence sources (informants), and interception of communications.
* **Data Protection Act 2018 & UK GDPR:** Legally require state authorities to process citizens' data lawfully, transparently, and only for specific, justified purposes.

---

**Primary Methods of Monitoring**

* **Communications Data & Internet Connection Records (ICRs):** Under the IPA, Telecommunication Service Providers (TSPs) and Internet Service Providers (ISPs) can be required to retain internet connection records (websites visited, connection times, metadata) for up to 12 months. Law enforcement can access *who* communicated with *whom* and *when*, but reading the actual *content* of messages requires a judicial warrant.
* **Targeted & Bulk Interception:** Agencies like GCHQ (Government Communications Headquarters), MI5, and specialized police units can conduct targeted interception (wiretaps, email monitoring) with a warrant approved by both a Secretary of State and an independent Judicial Commissioner.
* **CCTV & Automatic Number Plate Recognition (ANPR):**
* **CCTV:** Public authorities and local police use extensive networks of Closed-Circuit Television in public spaces. They are regulated by the Surveillance Camera Code of Practice.
* **ANPR:** The national ANPR camera network tracks vehicle movements on roads across the country to assist police in identifying stolen cars or tracing suspect vehicles involved in crime.


* **Facial Recognition Technology (LFR):** Some police forces, such as the Metropolitan Police and South Wales Police, deploy Live Facial Recognition cameras in public spaces to scan crowds against specific watchlists of wanted suspects or missing persons.
* **Bulk Personal Datasets (BPDs):** Security agencies hold databases containing large volumes of personal data (such as financial records, travel movements, or public registries) to assist in identifying individuals involved in serious crime or security threats.

---

**Oversight and Accountability**

* **Investigatory Powers Commissioner's Office (IPCO):** Provides independent oversight of how intelligence agencies and public bodies use surveillance powers, ensuring warrants adhere to the law.
* **Investigatory Powers Tribunal (IPT):** An independent judicial court where members of the public can submit complaints if they believe their communications or privacy have been unlawfully spied on by government agencies.
* **Information Commissioner's Office (ICO):** Regulates compliance with data protection laws, ensuring personal data collected by public authorities is handled securely and lawfully.
