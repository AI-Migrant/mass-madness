# UK Government Use of AI for Digital Surveillance and Manipulation

*Research Report | August 27, 2026*

---

## Question

How does the UK government use artificial intelligence for digital surveillance and manipulation, what forms do these uses take, and what are the legal, ethical, and human rights implications?

---

## Executive Summary

### Top 7 Takeaways

1. **Mass Facial Recognition Expansion**: The UK is rapidly expanding live facial recognition (LFR) surveillance, with plans to increase from 10 to over 50 vans nationwide by 2026, using AI software from Corsight AI—a company linked to Israel's controversial surveillance operations in Gaza. This £20 million rollout is part of a broader £115 million investment in police AI systems.

2. **Bias and Discrimination Embedded**: AI systems used for immigration and policing demonstrate systemic bias. The Home Office's visa streaming algorithm (abandoned in 2020 after legal challenge) automatically assigned "red" risk scores to entire nationalities, while current facial recognition shows higher false positive rates for Black and Asian individuals—up to 9.9% for Black women at certain thresholds.

3. **Intelligence Agency AI Surveillance**: GCHQ has placed AI at the heart of its operations, using it to analyze vast datasets, detect disinformation, map trafficking networks, and track child abusers. The Investigatory Powers Act 2016 provides legal basis for bulk data collection, with over 50 authorities able to access internet connection records without warrants.

4. **Military Information Warfare**: The 77th Brigade, a British Army unit specializing in psychological operations, explicitly includes AI experts and "mis-information" researchers among its personnel, indicating state involvement in AI-enabled information warfare and social media manipulation.

5. **Predictive Policing at Scale**: UK police forces deploy approximately 100 AI tools for predictive analytics, with the College of Policing advocating for rapid AI adoption. However, concerns persist about data bias, lack of transparency, and the ethical implications of algorithmic decision-making in law enforcement.

6. **Immigration Enforcement Automation**: The Home Office uses AI systems like IPIC (Identify and Prioritise Immigration Cases) to manage ~41,000 removal cases, while the forthcoming Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) system will rely on automated AI decisions, with non-compliance becoming a criminal offense from 2026.

7. **Weak Regulatory Oversight**: Despite public consultations and proposed frameworks, the UK lacks comprehensive, specific legislation governing AI surveillance. The current "patchwork" of laws has been criticized by human rights organizations, with the Court of Appeal ruling in 2020 that covert automated facial recognition violates human rights.

## Findings

### 1. Facial Recognition: The New Normal in Public Surveillance

#### Scale and Scope
The UK is undergoing a **national expansion of live facial recognition (LFR)** surveillance. In January 2026, Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood announced that the current fleet of 10 LFR vans would expand to **more than 50**, deployed nationwide to identify individuals on police watchlists. This rollout follows a six-month trial by Essex Police and represents a **£20 million investment** as part of the broader £115 million AI and automation funding for policing announced in the Police Reform White Paper.

Throughout 2024, LFR systems scanned **4.6 million faces** across England and Wales, while retrospective facial recognition (RFR) was used **252,798 times**. Police forces primarily use software from private sector companies including NEC, Cognitec, and Idemia.

#### Controversial Suppliers
Investigations by Al Jazeera confirmed that **Corsight AI**, an Israeli-based firm, had been subcontracted by UK company Digital Barriers to provide the facial recognition software. Corsight's technology has been **deployed in Gaza by Israeli cyber-intelligence division Unit 8200**, where it has been used to track, trace, and abduct Palestinian civilians at checkpoints. Israeli security officials have expressed concerns about its accuracy, including wrongful arrests and detentions of hundreds of Palestinians.

Corsight's board includes former Israeli intelligence officers: Igal Raichelgauz (former Israeli intelligence), Yaron Ashkenazi (former Shin Bet), and retired Major-General Giora Eiland, who is associated with the so-called "General's Plan" to isolate and starve northern Gaza.

#### Accuracy and Bias Concerns
While the Metropolitan Police reported a **0.0003% false positive rate** at a 0.6 threshold in 2024-25 (2,067 true alerts out of 2,077), independent testing reveals significant disparities:

- Tests on RFR algorithms in 2025 showed **higher false positive rates for Black and Asian people**
- One FRT system used by UK police showed **black women had the highest percentage (9.9%) of false positive identifications at a 0.8 threshold**
- Error rates increase by **up to 9.3%** when used outside testing environments (crowded places, poor lighting, partial face coverage)

In January 2026, RFR technology was **implicated in the wrongful arrest of an Asian man**, highlighting the real-world consequences of algorithmic bias.

#### Regulatory Framework
There is **no primary legislation specific to facial recognition technology**. Governance relies on:
- Common law and existing legislation
- College of Policing's Authorised Professional Practice
- Oversight by Biometrics and Surveillance Camera Commissioner (England & Wales) and Scottish Biometrics Commissioner

A **government consultation** to develop a new legal framework for facial recognition ended in February 2026, aiming to consolidate the current "patchwork" of laws. The Ada Lovelace Institute has recommended a **centralized approach** with a comprehensive legal framework.

In 2020, the **Court of Appeal ruled** that covert use of automated FRT does not comply with the European Convention on Human Rights, requiring police to notify the public when using LFR.

#### Future Plans
- **National rollout** of LFR to every regional police force in England and Wales
- **First permanent facial recognition cameras** installed in South London (October 2025)
- **Immigration Enforcement** expanding LFR use at UK ports to identify individuals in breach of deportation orders
- **Police.AI** national center integrating AI into LFR and other policing functions

### 2. Predictive Policing and AI Analytics in Law Enforcement

#### Current Deployment
The **College of Policing**, led by CEO Andy Marsh (former Avon and Somerset Chief Constable), is examining **around 100 currently deployed AI tools** for predictive policing. Marsh has stated that "effective AI should be injected like heroin" to speed up British police work, and that his organization's role is to "test the ones that work properly... and then spread them like wildfire through policing."

#### Specific Applications
- **Think Family Database**: A police data scientist developed predictive risk models to identify children at risk of sexual exploitation, drawing on datasets from police, councils, and public agencies
- **Crime prediction systems**: WIRED investigation revealed concerns about accuracy and bias in predictive analytics across the criminal justice system

#### Investment and Infrastructure
The government's **Police Reform White Paper** commits to:
- **£115 million investment** over 3 years for AI and automation in policing
- **£26 million** specifically for developing a national facial recognition system
- **Total package of £141 million** when combined with other AI initiatives
- Creation of **Police.AI** National Centre for AI in Policing
- Goal: Free up **6 million policing hours** each year

#### Ethical and Bias Concerns
Critics highlight:
- **"There's a lot of bias in the police's data"** — data scientist quoted in WIRED investigation
- **"Too many issues for it to be done ethically and fairly"** — concerns about algorithmic decision-making
- Need for **public information** on why and how predictive processes are carried out
- **Feedback loop problems**: Past bias in data reinforces future discrimination (identified in visa algorithm case)

### 3. Intelligence Agency AI: GCHQ and Mass Surveillance

#### GCHQ's AI Strategy
GCHQ has explicitly stated that **"AI capabilities will be at the heart of our future ability to protect the UK."** In its paper *Ethics of AI: Pioneering a New National Security*, the agency outlines AI's role in:

- **Pattern detection**: Helping analysts manage ever-increasing volume and complexity of data
- **Disinformation detection**: Spotting fake online messages used by foreign states
- **Trafficking network mapping**: Identifying individuals, accounts, and transactions in human/drug trafficking networks
- **Child sexual abuse investigation**: Analyzing grooming evidence, tracking disguised identities, discovering hidden services on the dark web
- **Cyber threat identification**: Detecting malicious software and attackers

GCHQ has established an **industry-facing AI lab** in Manchester, dedicated to prototyping projects and mentoring start-ups.

#### Adversary AI Use
GCHQ warns that adversaries are already using AI to:
- **Automate disinformation production** at scale
- Create **deepfake video and audio**
- **Personally target and customize** false content
- Spread disinformation through **chatbots** or by **interfering with social media algorithms**
- Operate **troll farms** and **botnet accounts**

#### Limitations
GCHQ acknowledges that AI **cannot predict human behavior** such as terrorist attack execution, emphasizing the need for human oversight.

#### Investigatory Powers Act 2016
The **"Snooper's Charter"** provides the legal framework for mass surveillance:

**Bulk Powers:**
- Bulk collection of communications data
- Bulk interception of communications
- Bulk equipment interference (hacking) for national security
- Retention of Internet Connection Records (ICRs) for 1 year

**Authorities with Access:** Over 50 bodies can access ICRs **without a warrant**, including:
- All police forces (Metropolitan, City of London, regional forces)
- Intelligence agencies (MI5, MI6, GCHQ)
- Government departments (Home Office, MoD, HMRC, DWP, etc.)
- Various other public authorities

**Oversight:**
- Investigatory Powers Commissioner (judicial oversight)
- Intelligence and Security Committee of Parliament
- Investigatory Powers Tribunal
- **Amended in 2024** following review by Lord Anderson

**Controversies:**
- **Apple Inc.** received a technical capability notice in February 2025 ordering them to break iCloud encryption worldwide
- Privacy campaigners argue the Act provides **"mass surveillance powers"** comparable to authoritarian regimes
- Court rulings have found aspects violate EU law, requiring amendments

### 4. Information Warfare and Manipulation: The 77th Brigade

#### Structure and Capabilities
The **77th Brigade** is a British Army formation specializing in **information operations and psychological warfare**, created in January 2015 and operational from April 2015. Based at Denison Barracks in Hermitage, Berkshire, the brigade initially brought together:

- A psychological warfare unit
- A media operations unit
- The Military Stabilisation and Support Group (MSSG)

Crucially, the brigade's personnel include **experts in photography, journalism, marketing, social media, AI, academia & research (mis-information), and graphic design**.

#### AI and Misinformation Operations
The explicit inclusion of **AI experts and "mis-information" researchers** within the 77th Brigade indicates that the UK military is developing capabilities in:
- **AI-enabled psychological operations**
- **Social media manipulation**
- **Information warfare**
- **Disinformation campaigns**

This represents a **state-level investment in AI for strategic communication and influence operations**, though specific operational details remain classified.

#### Context
The brigade was formed as part of the Army 2020 reorganization following the Strategic Defence and Security Review 2010, suggesting a long-term strategic commitment to information operations.

### 5. Immigration and Border Control: AI-Driven Enforcement

#### Visa Streaming Algorithm (Abandoned 2020)
The Home Office's **visa streaming algorithm** was used for years to process every visa application to the UK until it was abandoned in August 2020 following a legal challenge by Foxglove and the Joint Council for the Welfare of Immigrants (JCWI).

**Key Findings:**
- **Secret list of "suspect nationalities"** automatically given a 'red' traffic-light risk score
- **Entrenched racism and bias** into the visa system
- **Feedback loop problems**: Past bias and discrimination fed into the algorithm reinforced future bias
- Academic and nonprofit organizations **stopped attempting** to have colleagues from certain countries visit the UK
- **First successful judicial review** of a UK government algorithmic decision-making system

The case revealed an **alarming lack of transparency** about where AI tools are used and a **lack of safeguards** to prevent biased software from ruining lives.

#### Warnings Index
The **Warnings Index** is a UK immigration watchlist database providing law enforcement agencies (Border Force, police) with names of individuals:
- With previous UK immigration history
- Of interest to detection staff
- Of interest for matters of national security

The Home Office has **refused to disclose details** of the unit's activity, but it is known to be involved in at least two projects: the Warnings Index and a status checking project.

Critics argue this represents an attempt to build a **"massive migrant database"** without learning lessons from the Windrush Scandal.

#### IPIC System
The **AI-powered Identify and Prioritise Immigration Cases (IPIC)** system plays a **central role** in Home Office enforcement processes, managing the cases of approximately **41,000 people facing potential removal from the UK**.

#### Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA)
From 2026, the **ETA system** will be fully enforced, making it a **criminal offense** to knowingly travel to the UK without an ETA in place. The system relies on **automated AI decision-making**, increasing the stakes for reliable decision-making and raising questions about **transparency and human oversight**.

As AI reshapes the immigration system, legal professionals warn that **complex processes requiring human intelligence** (such as visa approvals) may be increasingly automated, **magnifying risks of error and lack of accountability**.

### 6. Legal and Regulatory Framework

#### Online Safety Act 2023
The Online Safety Act imposes duties on service providers regarding:
- **Illegal content**
- **Content harmful to children**

**AI Provisions:**
- Section 192 allows provider judgments about content to be made **"by human moderators, by means of automated systems or processes, or by means of automated systems or processes together with human moderators"**
- Ofcom is consulting on **proactive technologies** for content moderation
- **Criticism**: Parliamentary committees argue the Act **fails to tackle viral spread of misinformation** or algorithmic amplification of false content
- The Act **does not adequately cover generative AI platforms**

#### AI Regulation Evolution
- **2023**: UK rejected horizontal AI regulation, favoring sector-specific approach
- **July 2024**: King's Speech proposed **binding measures on AI**, marking a shift from non-binding approach
- **2025**: AI Safety Institute renamed to **AI Security Institute**, signaling focus shift from ethics to security
- **February 2026**: Government consultation on new legal framework for facial recognition concluded

#### Proposed Reforms
- Ada Lovelace Institute recommends **centralized approach** to biometric governance
- Need for **comprehensive legal framework** providing clarity on limits, lawfulness, and proportionality

### 7. Human Rights and Civil Liberties Concerns

#### International Criticism
- **Amnesty International**: UK government has **"clear legal obligations to help prevent and punish genocide"** and is **"scandalously failing to meet its responsibilities"** by partnering with Corsight AI
- **Human Rights Watch**: UK's facial recognition rollout represents **"sacrificing human rights on a countrywide scale"**
  - **"Mass violation of privacy and non-discrimination rights"**
  - Puts at risk **freedom of expression, association, and assembly**
  - **"Taxpayer money should not be spent contracting companies that are facilitating human rights abuses"**

#### Domestic Concerns
- **Baroness Casey Review (2023)**: Metropolitan Police (largest FRT user) has **"institutional homophobia, misogyny and racism, and other forms of discrimination"**
- **Equality and Human Rights Commission (EHRC)**: Challenged Met Police's FRT use, highlighting **potential human rights effects if misused**
- **Public opinion**: While 64% support FRT in policing, concerns persist about **misuse, data security, and false identification**

#### Specific Incidents
- **January 2026**: RFR technology implicated in **wrongful arrest of an Asian man**
- **2024**: Police forces conducted FRT searches using **passport database** (45+ million people), described as a **"breach of the right to privacy"** by campaigners

## Open Questions

### Unresolved Issues

1. **Transparency Gaps**: What specific AI tools are deployed across government departments, and where are they used? The lack of a central registry hinders public oversight.

2. **Classification Limits**: To what extent are military and intelligence AI programs (beyond what's publicly acknowledged) being used for surveillance and manipulation?

3. **Algorithmic Accountability**: How can individuals challenge AI-driven decisions that affect their rights, particularly in immigration and policing contexts?

4. **Cross-Border Data Sharing**: How does UK AI surveillance integrate with Five Eyes partners and other international intelligence-sharing arrangements?

5. **Future Legislation**: Will the UK adopt comprehensive AI regulation, or continue its sector-specific approach? What will the new facial recognition legal framework contain?

6. **Private Sector Role**: What safeguards exist for the increasing reliance on private companies (Digital Barriers, Corsight AI, NEC, etc.) for core surveillance functions?

### Emerging Threats

1. **Deepfake Proliferation**: As deepfake technology improves (crossing a "critical threshold" in 2026), how will the UK government both combat and potentially exploit synthetic media?

2. **AI Arms Race**: How will GCHQ's acknowledgment that adversaries use AI for disinformation influence UK's own offensive information operations?

3. **Automation Creep**: Will complex immigration and legal decisions increasingly be automated, and what safeguards will prevent errors and lack of accountability?

4. **Surveillance Normalization**: As facial recognition becomes ubiquitous, what prevents mission creep from targeted policing to mass surveillance?

## Recommendations

### For Policymakers

1. **Enact Comprehensive AI Legislation**: Replace the current patchwork with a unified legal framework that explicitly addresses surveillance AI, requiring:
   - Mandatory human rights impact assessments
   - Transparency requirements for AI systems used in public decision-making
   - Clear prohibitions on biased and discriminatory applications

2. **Strengthen Oversight**: Expand the role and resources of the Biometrics and Surveillance Camera Commissioner to cover all AI surveillance technologies, not just facial recognition.

3. **Ban High-Risk Applications**: Prohibit AI systems that:
   - Automatically assign risk scores based on nationality or ethnicity
   - Enable mass surveillance without individualized suspicion
   - Use data from companies implicated in human rights abuses

4. **Public Registry**: Create a mandatory public registry of all AI systems deployed by government agencies, including purpose, data sources, and oversight mechanisms.

### For Civil Society

1. **Legal Challenges**: Continue strategic litigation against discriminatory AI systems, building on the successful visa algorithm case.

2. **Monitoring and Reporting**: Establish independent monitoring of AI surveillance deployment, particularly in policing and immigration contexts.

3. **Public Awareness**: Educate the public about AI surveillance capabilities and their rights regarding data and privacy.

4. **Technical Audits**: Demand independent technical audits of government AI systems, with results made public.

### For Technology Community

1. **Ethical Guidelines**: Develop and enforce ethical guidelines for AI companies supplying surveillance technology to governments.

2. **Bias Mitigation**: Prioritize research and development of bias detection and mitigation techniques for surveillance AI.

3. **Transparency Tools**: Create tools that allow individuals to understand and challenge AI-driven decisions affecting them.

### For International Community

1. **Export Controls**: Implement international controls on the export of surveillance AI technology to prevent human rights abuses.

2. **Information Sharing**: Establish mechanisms for sharing best practices and warnings about problematic AI surveillance vendors.

3. **Human Rights Framework**: Develop an international framework for the ethical use of AI in surveillance and law enforcement.
