# How UK Supermarkets Help Authorities Create Negative Impressions Against Targets

*Deep Research Report | August 28, 2026*

---

## Question

How do supermarkets in the United Kingdom collaborate with state authorities to create, reinforce, or amplify negative public impressions and official narratives against targeted individuals and groups?

---

## Executive Summary

UK supermarkets actively collaborate with police and Home Office authorities through extensive data-sharing and surveillance partnerships that extend beyond crime prevention, enabling authorities to construct and disseminate negative impressions against targeted populations. The mechanisms include:

1. **Project Pegasus**: A £600,000+ retailer-funded initiative where major supermarkets (Tesco, Sainsbury's, Co-op, Waitrose, John Lewis, Next, M&S, Boots, Primark) share CCTV imagery with police for facial recognition matching against the Police National Database, creating a national intelligence picture that frames certain individuals as prolific offenders.

2. **Facewatch System**: Private facial recognition watchlists (516,739 alerts issued in 2025) that allow supermarkets to secretly blacklist individuals—including those without criminal convictions—across multiple stores, with police also able to upload images, effectively creating a parallel surveillance network that stigmatizes targets.

3. **Home Office Data Access**: Government guidance mandates that surveillance footage must be made available to Home Office Immigration Enforcement within 24 hours of request, directly linking supermarket surveillance to immigration enforcement and border control narratives.

4. **Statistical Narrative Shaping**: The British Retail Consortium's crime reports (5.5 million shoplifting incidents, £2.2 billion in losses for 2023-24) are weaponized in media and policy debates to justify crackdowns, remove legal thresholds for prosecution, and paint retail crime as an existential crisis requiring expanded surveillance powers.

5. **Normalization of Surveillance**: The collaboration legitimizes and normalizes pervasive biometric surveillance, making it appear as a necessary and proportionate response to retail crime, thereby reducing public resistance to state surveillance expansion.

These mechanisms collectively enable authorities to construct negative impressions by: associating targets with criminality through watchlists and databases, using supermarket-provided data as authoritative evidence in policy justification, and framing surveillance as both inevitable and beneficial.

## Findings

### 1. Direct Surveillance Collaboration: Project Pegasus

**Mechanism**: Major UK supermarket chains collectively fund and participate in a national police operation that integrates private retail surveillance with state law enforcement databases.

**Key Facts**:
- **Participants**: John Lewis, Co-op, Tesco, Sainsbury's, Waitrose, Next, M&S, Boots, Primark, and others
- **Funding**: £600,000 initial investment from 10 retailers, with reports of £6 million total for the expanded Pegasus Partnership
- **Operation**: Police run supermarket CCTV images of suspected shoplifters through the Police National Database using facial recognition technology
- **Objective**: Build a "comprehensive intelligence picture of organised crime gangs" across the country
- **Government backing**: Spearheaded by Katy Bourne (APCC Business Crime lead) and backed by the Home Office
- **Specialist team**: New police unit of analysts and intelligence officers within OPAL (Organised Acquisitive Crime policing unit)

**Impression Creation Impact**:
- **Criminal association**: Individuals identified through this system are officially framed as part of "organised crime gangs" in police intelligence
- **National database integration**: Private supermarket footage becomes part of permanent police records, creating official documentation of criminal association
- **Media amplification**: Government press releases and retail industry statements portray this as a necessary response to an epidemic of retail crime, reinforcing the narrative of widespread lawlessness

**Sources**: [NPCC Press Release](https://news.npcc.police.uk/releases/partnership-to-crack-down-on-shoplifting), [Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202309/uk-police-retailers-partner-to-fight-shoplifting-with-biometrics), [The Mirror](https://www.mirror.co.uk/money/supermarkets-including-tesco-sainsburys-waitrose-30910933), [TopTenAIAgents](https://toptenaiagents.co.uk/blog/uk-ai-biometrics-2026-retail-surveillance-sme-guide.html)

### 2. Private Watchlist Systems: Facewatch and Retail Blacklisting

**Mechanism**: Supermarkets deploy live facial recognition (LFR) systems that create and share watchlists of "subjects of interest" across retail networks, with police also contributing to these lists.

**Key Facts**:
- **Scale**: 516,739 live facial recognition alerts issued via Facewatch in 2025 (more than double 2024)
- **Retail adoption**: Sainsbury's, Asda, and Tesco have accelerated LFR deployment across their UK estates
- **Watchlist scope**: Includes people suspected of shoplifting OR "disorderly conduct" by store staff
- **No criminal threshold**: Individuals can be added without any criminal conviction or formal charge
- **Cross-retailer sharing**: A person banned from one store can be denied entry at stores owned by entirely separate companies
- **Police integration**: Police have the power to upload images and data directly to Facewatch's system
- **External sources**: Watchlist also includes "subjects of interest" from police websites and Crimestoppers

**Impression Creation Impact**:
- **Secret blacklisting**: Individuals are added to watchlists without their knowledge or due process, creating a permanent digital stigma
- **Guilt by association**: Being flagged by the system creates an immediate negative impression for store staff and security
- **Disproportionate impact**: Facial recognition technology has documented issues with misidentifying people of colour, leading to wrongful accusations and negative impressions based on algorithmic bias
- **Permanent record**: Once added, biometric data can be retained and shared, creating a lasting negative association

**Privacy Violations & Complaints**:
- Big Brother Watch filed legal complaints against Southern Co-op and Facewatch with the ICO in July 2022
- Complaints allege the system is "Orwellian in the extreme, highly likely to be unlawful"
- Concerns raised about lack of transparency, no due process for watchlist inclusion, and potential for bias and discrimination
- ICO investigation found Facewatch's policies had breached data protection law, vindicating the legal complaint

**Sources**: [Big Brother Watch Complaint](https://bigbrotherwatch.org.uk/blog/update-big-brother-watchs-complaint-to-the-ico-on-retailer-facial-recognition/), [BBC News](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/uk-england-62297546), [Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/252523145/Supermarket-facial-recognition-challenged-by-privacy-group), [Coda Story](https://www.codastory.com/authoritarian-tech/uk-supermarket-biometric-cameras/)

### 3. Immigration Enforcement Data Sharing

**Mechanism**: Supermarket surveillance infrastructure is directly accessible to Home Office Immigration Enforcement for border control and immigration enforcement purposes.

**Key Facts**:
- **Mandatory sharing**: GOV.UK guidance states "All footage must be made available to the Home Office within 24 hours of a request"
- **Immigration enforcement access**: Home Office staff can request CCTV footage for immigration enforcement purposes
- **Border surveillance integration**: The Home Office uses surveillance data to prosecute under Sections 24 and 25 of the Immigration Act 1971
- **Data sharing protocols**: Government has established "Data Sharing Protocol with food retailers to regularly gather information on a voluntary basis"

**Impression Creation Impact**:
- **Criminalization of migration**: Supermarket surveillance data is used to support immigration prosecutions, associating retail spaces with border enforcement
- **Normalization of migration surveillance**: The routine sharing of retail surveillance with immigration authorities reinforces the narrative that immigration enforcement is a normal part of public safety
- **Targeted groups**: This creates negative impressions specifically against migrant communities and those perceived as foreign

**Sources**: [GOV.UK Surveillance Camera Systems](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/surveillance-camera-systems/surveillance-camera-systems-accessible), [Migrants Rights Network](https://migrantsrightsnetwork.substack.com/p/the-home-office-uses-border-surveillance), [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=96c187df-a49e-4b8a-ad9e-c7a50e8adff2)

### 4. Statistical Narrative Construction

**Mechanism**: Supermarket industry bodies produce crime statistics that are strategically deployed in media and policy debates to justify expanded surveillance and law enforcement powers.

**Key Facts**:
- **BRC Crime Survey 2025**: Reports retail crime at "highest level on record"
- **Scale**: 5.5 million detected shoplifting incidents in 2023-24, costing retailers £2.2 billion
- **Violence**: Over 2,000 incidents of violence or abuse per day against retail workers
- **Policy impact**: These statistics were used to justify:
  - Removing the £200 threshold for "low level" theft prosecution
  - Introducing standalone offence for assaulting retail workers (King's Speech 2025)
  - Crime and Policing Bill 2025 retail crime provisions
- **Media amplification**: BRC CEO Helen Dickinson's statement that "Retail crime is spiralling out of control" was widely reported, with descriptions of staff being "spat on, racially abused, and threatened with machetes"

**Impression Creation Impact**:
- **Crisis framing**: The statistics and language create an impression of retail crime as an out-of-control epidemic requiring extraordinary measures
- **Justification for surveillance**: The scale of reported incidents is used to justify Project Pegasus and expanded facial recognition deployment
- **Public perception shaping**: Media coverage of BRC reports creates a perception that shoplifting is a major societal problem, not just a retail industry issue
- **Policy legitimization**: Government uses these industry-provided statistics to justify legislation that expands state surveillance powers

**Sources**: [BRC Crime Survey 2025](https://brc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/operations/2025/ungated/brc-retail-crime-survey-2025/), [BRC Retail Crime Spiralling](https://brc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/corporate-affairs/2025/ungated/retail-crime-spiralling-out-of-control/), [GOV.UK Crime and Policing Bill Factsheet](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/crime-and-policing-bill-2025-factsheets/crime-and-policing-bill-retail-crime-factsheet)

### 5. Normalization and Legitimization of Surveillance

**Mechanism**: The supermarket-police partnerships serve to normalize and legitimize pervasive surveillance, reducing public resistance and creating acceptance of expanded state monitoring.

**Key Facts**:
- **Public-private partnership framing**: Surveillance is presented as a collaborative, beneficial effort rather than state overreach
- **Cost-effectiveness narrative**: Retailers and police frame facial recognition as a "cost-effective response to shoplifting" (Facewatch CEO)
- **ROI justification**: Surveillance vendors promise retailers a return on investment within 3 years
- **Safety framing**: Surveillance is consistently framed as necessary for "staff safety" and "customer protection"

**Impression Creation Impact**:
- **Surveillance as normal**: The widespread adoption by trusted retail brands makes surveillance seem routine and acceptable
- **Beneficial narrative**: The framing of surveillance as protecting staff and customers creates positive associations with monitoring
- **Reduced scrutiny**: The retail context (private property) reduces legal and public scrutiny compared to police-deployed surveillance
- **Precedent setting**: Retail surveillance normalizes facial recognition, making it easier to expand to other public spaces

**Sources**: [TechRound](https://techround.co.uk/news/facial-recognition-supermarkets-security-measure-privacy-invasion/), [Benchmark Magazine](https://benchmarkmagazine.com/supermarkets-turn-to-visible-surveillance-as-retail-crime-escalates/)

## Recommendations & Next Steps

### For Investigative Follow-Up
- **FOI requests**: Submit requests to police forces for statistics on supermarket-provided data used in investigations and prosecutions
- **Legal challenges**: Monitor outcomes of ongoing legal complaints against Facewatch and supermarket chains
- **Parliamentary questions**: Query MPs on the scope of supermarket-Home Office data sharing agreements
- **Whistleblower outreach**: Seek insiders from supermarket security teams or Facewatch for internal documentation

### For Policy Advocacy
- **Regulatory reform**: Advocate for stricter regulation of private facial recognition deployment, including mandatory transparency reports
- **Due process requirements**: Push for legal requirements that individuals be notified and have appeal rights when added to retail watchlists
- **Data minimization**: Demand limits on data retention periods and restrictions on sharing with third parties
- **Bias auditing**: Require regular independent audits of facial recognition systems for demographic bias

### For Public Awareness
- **Consumer education**: Inform the public about surveillance practices in retail spaces they frequent
- **Retailer accountability**: Pressure supermarkets to disclose their surveillance practices and data sharing policies
- **Media scrutiny**: Encourage investigative journalism into the actual effectiveness and impacts of these surveillance partnerships
