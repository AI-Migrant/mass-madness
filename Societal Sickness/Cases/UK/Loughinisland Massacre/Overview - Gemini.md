# Research Report: The Loughinisland Massacre, Police Collusion, and the Foundation of *No Stone Unturned*

## Executive Summary
This report provides a targeted investigation into the contextual background and official investigative findings that underpin the documentary film *No Stone Unturned* (2017). Specifically, it examines the June 18, 1994, Loughinisland massacre—where six Catholic civilians were murdered by Ulster Volunteer Force (UVF) paramilitaries—and details the systemic police collusion and cover-ups documented in the landmark 2016 Police Ombudsman for Northern Ireland (PONI) report by Dr. Michael Maguire. This body of evidence formed the core factual backing for the journalists who produced *No Stone Unturned*, which in turn led to significant state power abuse and retaliatory law enforcement actions against the filmmakers.

## 1. Contextualization: The 1994 Loughinisland Massacre & Allegations of Collusion

### 1.1 The Attack (June 18, 1994)
On the evening of Saturday, June 18, 1994, during the FIFA World Cup match between the Republic of Ireland and Italy, two masked gunmen belonging to the Ulster Volunteer Force (UVF)—a loyalist paramilitary group—entered The Heights Bar in the small, predominantly nationalist village of Loughinisland, County Down.

The gunmen fired indiscriminately into the pub using an assault rifle and a pistol. Six men were killed, and five others were wounded:
* **Deceased Victims:**
  * Barney Green (87) – one of the oldest victims of the Northern Ireland conflict.
  * Eamon Byrne (39)
  * Malcolm Jenkinson (52)
  * Daniel McCreanor (59)
  * Patrick O'Hare (35)
  * Adrian Rogan (34)

### 1.2 The Political & Security Climate
The attack took place during a bloody phase of "The Troubles," just months before the August 1994 Provisional IRA cease-fire and the subsequent loyalist cease-fire in October 1994. Loyalist paramilitaries were increasingly targeting nationalist civilian spaces in retaliation for IRA violence.

### 1.3 Immediate Suspicion and Long-Standing Allegations of Collusion
From the outset, local residents, families of the victims, and investigative observers raised serious concerns regarding the Royal Ulster Constabulary (RUC) investigation:
* **Forensic Failures:** Crucial evidence was mishandled or destroyed shortly after the attack, including the deliberate destruction of the getaway vehicle (a red Triumph Acclaim) by the police in 1998, prior to comprehensive modern DNA analysis.
* **Informant Protection:** Suspicion quickly arose that key suspects were active informers for RUC Special Branch and Military Intelligence, immunizing them from arrest and prosecution.
* **Foreknowledge & Inaction:** Evidence emerged suggesting that the security forces had prior intelligence regarding UVF weapons shipments and planned attacks in County Down but chose not to intervene or disrupt the cell.

## 2. Findings of the 2016 Police Ombudsman Report (Dr. Michael Maguire)

In June 2016, Dr. Michael Maguire, the Police Ombudsman for Northern Ireland (PONI), published a definitive 100-page report following an extensive re-investigation into the Loughinisland attack and the original RUC response.

### 2.1 Formal Determination of Collusion
Unlike earlier PONI reports (such as Al Hutchinson’s controversial 2011 report, which found operational failings but stopped short of citing collusion), Dr. Maguire unequivocally concluded that **collusion between elements of the state security apparatus and loyalist paramilitaries was a central feature of the Loughinisland murders**.

> *"I have no hesitation in concluding that collusion was a significant feature of the murder of six men in Loughinisland in June 1994."* — Dr. Michael Maguire, PONI (2016)

### 2.2 Key Specific Findings

#### A. Infiltration and State-Awareness of V4 Assault Rifles
* The weapons used in the attack—specifically VZ.58 (V4) assault rifles—were part of a massive illegal arms shipment smuggled into Northern Ireland from South Africa in late 1987 by loyalist agent Brian Nelson and others.
* The Ombudsman established that security forces (RUC Special Branch and Military Intelligence) had advance knowledge of the shipment, permitted its entry to protect high-level intelligence sources, and allowed these weapons to be distributed across UVF and UDA units.
* These imported arms were subsequently linked to over 70 murders and attempted murders, including Loughinisland.

#### B. Destruction and Suppression of Critical Evidence
* **Getaway Car:** The red Triumph Acclaim used by the killers was disposed of by police while the investigation was ongoing, precluding advanced forensic testing.
* **Interview Records & Notebooks:** RUC Special Branch records, original interview notes, and police pocketbooks relating to key suspects vanished or were destroyed.
* **Hair Samples and DNA:** Key physical exhibits recovered from the scene were lost or never submitted for modern forensic comparison.

#### C. Protection of Informants Over Justice
* **Informant Immunity:** The report revealed that several primary suspects in the Loughinisland massacre were active police informants. RUC Special Branch withheld intelligence from the Senior Investigating Officer (SIO) handling the murder inquiry to protect these covert human intelligence sources (CHIS).
* **Delayed Arrests:** Arrests of key suspects were deliberately delayed for weeks after the attack, allowing individuals time to align alibis and dispose of secondary evidence.

#### D. Failure to Act on Warning Intelligence
* RUC Special Branch possessed actionable intelligence about UVF activities and planned attacks in the South Down region prior to June 18, 1994, but failed to put preventative security measures or targeted patrols in place.

## 3. Direct Linkage: From Official Findings to *No Stone Unturned* & State Retaliation

### 3.1 The Documentary *No Stone Unturned* (2017)
Directed by Academy Award-winner Alex Gibney and produced/researched by investigative journalists **Trevor Birney** and **Barry McCaffrey**, *No Stone Unturned* built directly upon Dr. Maguire’s 2016 Ombudsman findings.

While the Ombudsman report confirmed systemic collusion and identified suspects by code names/roles without publicly naming them due to legal constraints, Birney and McCaffrey utilized leaked confidential PONI documents and independent investigative journalism to:
1. **Name Primary Suspects:** The film publicly named the suspected gunman (a former soldier/loyalist paramilitary) and his wife, who had allegedly tipped off the police but whose information was ignored/suppressed.
2. **Expose the Depth of Cover-Up:** It mapped out the specific network of RUC Special Branch officers, British Military Intelligence handlers, and UVF operatives involved in maintaining the cover-up over two decades.

### 3.2 State Power Abuse and the Retaliatory Arrest of Journalists
The film's exposure of state complicity triggered an aggressive, heavy-handed response from law enforcement:
* **The August 2018 Police Raids:** Rather than investigating the collusion and original perpetrators exposed by the documentary, the Police Service of Northern Ireland (PSNI)—supported by Durham Constabulary—executed early-morning dawn raids on the homes and offices of journalists Trevor Birney and Barry McCaffrey.
* **Seizure of Materials:** Police seized millions of digital files, hard drives, notebooks, and phones, attempting to compel the disclosure of confidential journalistic sources and penalize the disclosure of state secrets.
* **Arrest & Bail:** Both journalists were arrested, handcuffed, and detained for over nine hours, facing potential prosecution under the Official Secrets Act.

### 3.3 Judicial Vindication and Abuse of Power Ruling (2019–2020)
The attempt to criminalize the investigative journalists backfired in the courts:
* **High Court Judgment (2019):** Lord Chief Justice Sir Declan Morgan ruled that the search warrants executed against Birney and McCaffrey were completely unlawful, stating that police had failed to respect the fundamental rights of investigative journalists to protect their sources under Article 10 of the ECHR.
* **Quashing of Investigation & Apology:** In 2020, the PSNI quashed the criminal investigation against the journalists, agreed to pay over £800,000 in damages and legal fees, and issued a formal, unreserved public apology to Birney and McCaffrey for the unlawful arrests and disruption.
